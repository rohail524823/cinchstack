Why this is so hard

Every “best CRM” list was written by the CRM.

Review sites are pay-to-play, listicles are affiliate farms, and the vendor's own pricing page is a riddle. So you keep the tabs open, and the decision keeps sliding a week.

the one you'd actually pick 47 tabs open “best CRM 2026” · sponsored 4.6 ★ from 12 reviews annual billing only seat minimum: 10 free trial ends tomorrow

The top result is an ad. The second is an affiliate farm. The third is the vendor comparing itself to its competitors.

Ratings are aggregated from people who used the free tier for a week, and from a few who were sent a gift card.

Nobody tells you the price at 40 seats, the annual lock-in, or the add-on you'll need by month three.

How CinchStack works

We buy the software, run it, and tell you what broke.

Not aggregated ratings, not a feature matrix scraped from marketing pages. A small number of tools, used properly, on a real workload — and one clear pick at the end.

01

We run it for real

Every tool in a guide gets set up on a real workload and used properly — long enough to hit the limits you'd hit in month two, not month zero.

02

We publish the tradeoffs

Including the ones vendors bury: the seat minimum, the annual-only discount, the feature that turns out to be a separate SKU.

03

You get a pick

Each guide ends with one recommendation, the team it's wrong for, and what we'd choose instead at three different sizes.

What makes it different

Six things a review site won't do.

01

We pay for everything

No free vendor accounts, no press units, no briefings in exchange for coverage. If we're writing about it, we bought it.

02

Rankings before links

The recommendation is settled and written down before anyone checks whether an affiliate programme exists. Where we earn, we mark it on the line where we earn it.

03

We publish who shouldn't buy

Every recommendation names the team it's wrong for. A pick that suits everyone is a pick that was never tested.

04

Prices are re-checked

SaaS pricing moves quietly. Each guide carries a visible last-verified date, and we re-check rather than let it rot.

05

Total cost, not sticker price

Seat minimums, annual lock-in, onboarding fees, and the add-on you'll need by month three — modelled at the size you'll actually be.

06

We show our method

The test workload, how we scored it, and what we got wrong last time. If you disagree with the pick, you can see exactly where.
