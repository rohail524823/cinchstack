## Is GoHighLevel a good CRM for real estate agents?

Yes, for the agent who makes their own leads. HighLevel's real estate page pitches listing pages, property funnels, home valuation campaigns and open house registration pages, with every sign-up flowing into the CRM and into workflows that send texts, emails, voicemail drops and appointment reminders on their own. The same page describes custom property records linked to buyer and seller contacts, and review requests sent after a closing. For a solo agent or a small team that runs its own ads and follow-up, that puts the website, the texting tool and the CRM in one account.

It is a weaker fit when the leads arrive from somewhere else. A team that pays for Zillow or Realtor.com leads, or captures buyers on an IDX home-search site, needs each of those leads pulled in and passed to the right agent quickly. Follow Up Boss is built around that job, while HighLevel's real estate page talks about leads from pages you build yourself rather than from portals.

Follow Up Boss is frank about its own edges. Its FAQ says it is not a lead provider or a website company, and that it is not for anyone generating fewer than 30 leads a month. So the deciding question is where your leads begin: on pages you build, or on portals and websites someone else runs for you.

Expect more setup work on GoHighLevel, since its website, forms and workflows are yours to build. Follow Up Boss's FAQ says connecting the lead providers you already use takes less than 10 minutes, though it leaves the website to another company. The FAQ on HighLevel's real estate page calls HighLevel a platform "built for agencies", and a lone agent still works from an agency account with a sub-account inside it. To shorten the job, HighLevel offers a Real Estate Agent Snapshot, a template for a sub-account with a website, forms, calendars, workflows, email campaigns, online ads and social posts; its help article was last updated in May 2024.

## Does GoHighLevel connect to Zillow, Realtor.com or an IDX website?

Not through any built-in connection that HighLevel's own pages describe. Its pricing page never mentions Zillow, Realtor.com, MLS data or IDX, and its real estate page mentions MLS listings and IDX only inside a customer testimonial, not in its own description of the product. No HighLevel help article we read for this guide describes a portal connection, an MLS feed or IDX home search either. On HighLevel's public ideas board, a request for an IDX integration, first posted in May 2020 and backed by 289 voters, still carried the status "Under review" when we checked on 6 October 2026.

What HighLevel does document is a general way in. Its inbound webhook, a premium workflow trigger, lets another system send data into your account; after the first 100 executions per sub-account, a one-time allowance rather than a monthly one, each run costs $0.01 from your wallet. Before you count on that route, ask the portal whether it can post leads to a webhook or whether you would need a connector service in between.

One add-on's name can mislead agents. HighLevel's Online Listings, $30 a month per sub-account with a 3-month commitment, has nothing to do with homes for sale: HighLevel's pricing guide describes it as keeping a business's address, operating hours and phone number consistent across 70+ websites.

Lead intake is where Follow Up Boss is strongest. Its pricing page lists IDX websites and Facebook Lead Ads among its integrations and includes unlimited lead sources on every plan, and its integrations page counts 200+ sources. Its help center documents a Realtor.com connection by API plus email parsing, and both one-way and two-way Zillow integrations, the two-way version syncing stages, assignments, notes and deals back to Zillow. For other providers, its FAQ describes setup as changing the email address they send leads to, and its tracking pixel, added to an IDX site, shows which properties a lead viewed. Follow Up Boss has belonged to Zillow Group since December 2023; its announcement says the customer data you enter stays in Follow Up Boss, and Zillow Group cannot use it without your permission.

## How do GoHighLevel and Follow Up Boss hand new leads to agents?

Follow Up Boss treats routing as a setting. Its lead routing page lists rules that send leads by ZIP code or town, pass a listing's inquiries to the listing agent and give high-priced leads to senior agents, with the same options for every lead source. A team can release leads first to claim, which alerts everyone at once and favors whoever can help first, or deal them out by round robin. Leads that go quiet can move to lead ponds, where other agents are free to prospect them.

GoHighLevel treats routing as a workflow step. HighLevel's Assign To User action gives each new contact to one user, or rotates contacts among several in round robin, evenly or by weight: a 5:2 split sends a senior agent 5 leads for every 2 a newer agent gets. A toggle limits it to contacts that have no owner yet. Narrower rules, such as one ZIP code's leads going to one agent, have to be built into the workflow around that step; for complicated rules, HighLevel's article shows outside software picking the agent and passing the choice back by inbound webhook. None of the HighLevel pages we saved describes a first-to-claim option or lead ponds.

Reporting splits the same way. Follow Up Boss's entry plan advertises reports on agent and lead source performance. HighLevel's core features include custom dashboards and reporting, but its pricing card lists "User/Agent Reporting" among the Agency Pro additions, so a team leader should ask HighLevel what Starter shows per agent before relying on it.

## What do texting and A2P registration involve on each platform?

Both platforms require A2P 10DLC registration before you text US mobile numbers from a local number, but they divide the paperwork and the fees differently.

On GoHighLevel, the business registers its own brand and campaign under A2P 10DLC and pays the fees: $22.50 once for a Sole Proprietor or Low Volume Standard brand with its first campaign, $15.00 for each further campaign, then a monthly campaign fee of $1.50 for Low Volume Mixed, $2.00 for a Sole Proprietor campaign or $10.00 for standard use cases. HighLevel's registration guide says a business with no Tax ID can register only a single number, and its fee article lists the one-number tier at 3,000 segments a day, against 6,000 for a Low Volume Standard brand.

Follow Up Boss calls the process Business Registration. Its help center says the account owner needs an EIN, that one registration covers every team member and every number added later, and that Follow Up Boss reviews the form, submits it and covers the registration fees. Its guide says the registry's review takes around 48 hours.

Volume is where the bills part. GoHighLevel meters every segment, sent or received, at $0.00747, plus a carrier fee such as $0.0045 for T-Mobile and Verizon customers. In our small-team bill, 1,000 texts a month add $7.47 in segment charges and $4.50 in carrier fees; at 5,000 texts, those two lines reach $37.35 and $22.50. Follow Up Boss's pricing page shows no per-text charge, and its Pro plan includes unlimited calling and texting for all users. Its help center says texts go out from the company number unless a user has the dialer, and its pricing table lists individual agent numbers under Integrated Calling, an added cost on Grow.

Automation differs as well. HighLevel's real estate page describes nurture texts that go out automatically. Follow Up Boss's FAQ says new leads can get an automatic first text from its Lead Flow page, but its Automations do not send drip texts, and its texting page points to partner services for drip campaigns. Both registrations ask how your contacts agreed to receive texts, so neither is a route to texting lists that never opted in. ## Which one fits an investor, a solo agent or a growing team?

- **A solo agent who builds their own pipeline** with a website, home valuation funnels, ads and texting: GoHighLevel Starter. If most of your leads come from Zillow or another portal and you already have a website, Follow Up Boss Grow without calling costs less than Starter.
- **A team working portal and IDX leads:** Follow Up Boss, on Grow while $69 a seat plus $39 for each agent with calling stays under Pro's $499, and on Pro after that, which happens at 8 agents even with no calling. Its routing rules, lead ponds and unmetered texting on Pro are what the per-seat price pays for.
- **A team that markets itself** with listing funnels, open house sign-ups and its own ads: GoHighLevel Starter, provided round-robin or weighted assignment covers your routing.
- **An investor or wholesaler** marketing to property owners: GoHighLevel. Its funnels, pipelines and texting cover outreach to owners who asked to hear from you; budget for texts at the per-segment rate and register at the tier your daily volume needs. Follow Up Boss's routing and portal integrations serve agents handling buyer and seller inquiries, which seller outreach rarely needs.
