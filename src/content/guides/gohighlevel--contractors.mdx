## Is GoHighLevel a good CRM for a roofing, HVAC or plumbing company?

For the customer side of the business, yes. HighLevel's playbook for home-service businesses sets out the routine it has in mind: capture the lead, turn the conversation into a booking, take the payment, ask for a review, then bring the customer back for the next job. All of it is in the <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" />-a-month Starter plan, with texts, calls, email and AI billed as you use them, and users are unlimited, so the owner, an office manager and two estimators all log in for that one price.

These are the features contractors lean on, as HighLevel's help center describes them:

- **Missed Call Text Back.** When a call rings past the timeout you set, GoHighLevel texts the caller automatically, after hours included; HighLevel suggests a timeout of 10 to 20 seconds. The text fires on every missed call, even when one person dials several times in a few minutes, so HighLevel recommends running it from a workflow recipe with a wait step.
- **Google Business Profile call tracking.** One switch puts a LeadConnector number from your account in the listing's main phone slot, moves your own number to the second slot and forwards calls to it. Calls to the listing's main number are then logged, and an unanswered one can set off the text back.
- **Follow-up and booking.** Workflows send a new lead a sequence of texts and emails, and booking calendars hold the inspection or estimate visit.
- **Estimates and invoices.** You send an estimate by email or text, the customer accepts or declines it, and an accepted estimate converts into an invoice. Payment runs through a connected processor such as Stripe, PayPal or Square.
- **Review requests.** Once your Google Business Profile is connected, a request goes out by text or email, sent by hand from Quick Actions or the Reputation tab, or from a workflow step.

The gaps matter as much as the list. GoHighLevel's pricing page names no crew scheduling, routing, job costing or time tracking among its features.

## Where is Jobber the stronger tool for a contractor?

Jobber is built around the job itself. Its pricing page lists roofing, HVAC, plumbing and electrical among the trades it serves, and its plan cards, as saved on 6 October 2026, add features in steps:

- **Core**, $49 a month for one user on monthly billing: online booking and scheduling, quotes, invoices with online payment, a website and reporting.
- **Connect**, $139 a month for one user: automated client reminders, automatic payment collection, job checklists, automatic quote and invoice follow-ups, QuickBooks Online sync, and time and expense tracking.
- **Grow**, $199 a month for one user: quotes with visuals and reviews, optional line items for upsells, automatic time tracking, job costing, two-way texting and custom workflow automations.

Teams pay by size. Jobber's team-size menu runs from one person to 16 or more, and the 2-to-5-person tier, which includes 5 users, starts at Connect for $199 a month, with Grow at $299. The top plan at that size, Plus, costs $499 a month through Jobber's sales team and adds crew scheduling, unlimited job photos and video, profitability alerts and three add-ons: Pipeline, Marketing Suite and Receptionist. On every plan, Jobber's payment rates are 2.9% + 30¢ per card charge, 2.7% + 30¢ for tap-to-pay and 1% for bank (ACH) payments.

Online booking, quotes, invoices, payments and a website overlap with GoHighLevel, which also documents a two-way QuickBooks integration of its own. The difference is the work between a signed quote and a paid invoice. A plumbing company with four vans needs crew schedules, routes, job checklists, time tracking and job costing to see which jobs were profitable. Jobber's feature table lists all five, though job costing starts at Grow and crew scheduling comes only with Plus; GoHighLevel's feature list includes none of them. For running the work itself, Jobber is the better tool.

## How do GoHighLevel and Jobber compare on missed calls, texting and reviews?

This is where the two overlap, and they charge for it in opposite ways. GoHighLevel includes these features in every plan and bills each message from a prepaid wallet; Jobber puts them in higher plans or sells them as add-ons.

- **Missed calls.** Missed Call Text Back is a phone-system setting on every GoHighLevel plan. Jobber's Receptionist, a $29-a-month add-on, has an AI answer calls and texts around the clock, book visits into Jobber's schedule and text back callers who hang up, which is more than a text back. GoHighLevel's nearest match, Voice AI, is metered by use or sold through a $50 or $97 monthly AI Employee plan per location, and call charges apply either way.
- **Two-way texting.** Every GoHighLevel plan has it. On Jobber it starts with Grow, while Connect sends automated reminders and follow-ups by text or email.
- **Review requests.** Every GoHighLevel plan sends them, by hand or from a workflow. Jobber's Marketing Suite, a $99-a-month add-on, requests a review automatically after every job and drafts replies with AI. Jobber's marketing page adds that a Jobber website and Google Business Profile optimization come with every plan at no extra cost.
- **Lead pipeline.** GoHighLevel's CRM and pipelines are core features. Jobber's Pipeline add-on, $49 a month, gathers open requests and quotes in one view.

On GoHighLevel, the messages are the cost: $0.00747 per US text segment, sent or received, plus a carrier fee of $0.0035 to $0.0050 per segment sent, or $0.675 per 1,000 emails. Jobber's automatic review request has one real edge: Jobber knows when a job is finished. In GoHighLevel alone, a review request waits for someone to press the button or for a workflow you build to send it, though HighLevel's QuickBooks integration can send one when a customer's first invoice is paid in full.

## When is GoHighLevel enough on its own for a contractor?

When the bottleneck is leads, not logistics. A roofer with one crew selling a few replacement jobs a month, or a plumber working alone, can run the business from one GoHighLevel pipeline: stages for inspection booked, estimate sent, signed, scheduled, complete and paid, with calendars for the visits, estimates that turn into invoices and a review request at the end. CinchStack's solo bill for GoHighLevel, with 200 texts and 2,000 emails a month, comes to <Real tool="gohighlevel" size="solo" />, and our [local-service stack](/stacks/local-service/) shows what a website and the rest of a one-location setup add.

The signs that you need field-service software are practical: two crews working at once, jobs that take several visits, technicians who need a daily route and checklist, payroll that runs on timesheets, or no clear view of which jobs were profitable. At that point, add a field-service tool rather than stretching GoHighLevel's calendars into a dispatch board.

## When does a contractor need only Jobber?

When the schedule is already full and most work comes back on its own. An HVAC company with maintenance agreements, or a plumber living on repeat customers and referrals, has a dispatch problem more than a lead problem. Jobber's Connect plan handles reminders and follow-ups, Grow adds two-way texting, and the Receptionist and Marketing Suite add-ons cover missed calls and review requests. For a team of 2 to 5, Plus bundles both add-ons with Pipeline, and adding GoHighLevel on top would mostly pay twice for the same front end.

GoHighLevel earns a seat beside Jobber when the front end is busy: buying leads or running ads, building a landing page for each campaign, tracking which source each call came from, or nurturing estimates that sit for weeks before a homeowner decides.

## What does a contractor need before texting customers from GoHighLevel?

Before GoHighLevel can text a US mobile from a local number, the business has to pass A2P 10DLC registration: a brand, then a campaign, reviewed through The Campaign Registry. Missed Call Text Back depends on it too. HighLevel's guide says that without a number eligible to send texts, the attempt fails and the reason appears in the conversation. Toll-free numbers go through a separate verification instead.

HighLevel's A2P fee article lists these charges:

- **Once:** $22.50 for a Sole Proprietor or Low Volume Standard brand, including a $3 Fast Track fee aimed at approval within 3 business days, plus $15.00 for each further campaign.
- **Every month:** $1.50 for a Low Volume Mixed campaign, $2.00 for a Sole Proprietor campaign or $10.00 for standard use cases. HighLevel's phone pricing guide says submitting a campaign starts the registration fee and the monthly fee, whatever the review decides.

Your paperwork picks the tier. Without a business tax ID, HighLevel offers only single-number registration, the Sole Proprietor tier in its fee table, with 1 number and 3,000 segments a day. A Low Volume Standard brand allows several numbers and 6,000 segments a day, ample for a contractor's reminders and follow-ups.

Fix the website before you apply. HighLevel's campaign guide asks for a live site showing your business name and contact details, a privacy policy, terms and conditions, sample messages with opt-out wording, and a description of how customers agree to receive texts, such as a consent checkbox on your quote-request form. After approval, confirm the number carries the A2P Verified label under Phone Numbers. Then budget $1.15 a month for the local number, $0.02 a minute for calls forwarded to your cell, and the per-text rates in our [GoHighLevel SMS and calling cost guide](/tools/gohighlevel/sms-and-calling-costs/).
