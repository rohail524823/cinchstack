## What does a clinic, salon or trades business actually need running?

A clinic, salon, trades company or studio running on appointments needs the same four things whether it is a solo practitioner or a fifteen-person team: a way to take a booking and remember who made it, a way to nudge that person by text or email before and after the visit, a place those visits turn into a Google review, and a website where a new customer finds the business in the first place. <T id="gohighlevel" /> covers the first three almost entirely: its booking calendars, CRM, review requests and email and SMS follow-up sit inside one subscription, so a finished appointment can trigger the same kind of follow-up a much bigger business runs, without hiring anyone to send it by hand. <T id="squarespace" /> fills the fourth need, the public website, because GoHighLevel's own page builder is built for funnels and landing pages rather than the kind of content-forward site a service-area business needs to be found through. A rank-tracking tool for local search is the one piece worth holding off on; it earns its cost only once a business is provably running more than a single location, which the sections below cover in detail. A roofing, HVAC or plumbing company that also dispatches crews should read our [GoHighLevel for contractors guide](/tools/gohighlevel/contractors/), which shows where Jobber fits beside it. At one location, the whole stack runs <Stack id="local-service" size="solo" /> a month for a solo operator and <Stack id="local-service" size="small" /> a month for a five-person shop.

## Is GoHighLevel worth the metered texting and email charges?

Yes, for almost any clinic, salon or trades business that wants booking, reviews and text follow-up under one login, but the <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" /> sticker price is not the real number. Texts, calls, phone numbers and most email are billed separately from a prepaid wallet: a solo operator sending a light volume of appointment reminders and review requests pays <Real tool="gohighlevel" size="solo" /> a month once that usage is added, and a five-person shop's real bill runs <Real tool="gohighlevel" size="small" /> a month. Two catches the pricing page does not put in headline type: sending texts to US numbers first needs a one-time A2P 10DLC registration, and the wallet's auto-recharge cannot be switched off, so the card on file gets charged automatically whenever the balance runs low. The cheapest texting job here, an automatic reply to every unanswered call, is priced call by call in our [GoHighLevel missed call text back guide](/tools/gohighlevel/missed-call-text-back/). Neither obvious alternative wins on price by dropping features: <T id="pipedrive" /> and <T id="zoho-crm" /> both sell a real CRM, but neither sells a native texting feature at all, so a business that picks either one is signing up to handle appointment reminders and review requests by hand. <T id="hubspot" /> is the other name generic advice reaches for, and it is the wrong one here: its own pricing page sells marketing SMS only as an add-on to Marketing Hub Professional, priced at more than eight times GoHighLevel's Starter plan before the SMS add-on itself is even added.

## Squarespace or Wix: which website fits better?

Both builders are built for exactly this kind of business, but they make different trade-offs. <Price tool="squarespace" plan="basic" /> a month is Squarespace's entry price, published as a real month-to-month rate, and it caps site editors at two, the owner included; move to <Price tool="squarespace" plan="core" /> a month once a third person needs access, and every sale through the site itself carries no Squarespace transaction fee from there up. <Price tool="wix" plan="light" field="annualMonthly" /> a month looks cheaper, but that is Wix's only published rate for its Light plan: it is an annual price paid upfront, and Wix shows no month-to-month figure for Light anywhere on its own site. Light also excludes online payments entirely, so a salon that wants to take a deposit through its own site would need Wix's Core plan instead. The honest default is Squarespace, for the published monthly rate alone; pick Wix if the site is a pure brochure, online payments are not needed yet, and paying a year upfront is not a problem. A multi-location business that wants a real page per location, with its own hours, staff and photos managed like blog posts, is the one case for <T id="webflow" />: its <Price tool="webflow" plan="premium" /> plan carries a genuine content-managed system that a page-builder site cannot match once there is more than one address to keep current.

## Do you need SEO software to rank for "near me" searches?

Not at one location. A complete Google Business Profile, kept current with hours, photos and services, plus the reviews GoHighLevel's follow-up workflow already requests, do more for local search rank than a paid tool a solo operator or a five-person shop would open once a month. Google Search Console is free and catches basic indexing problems, which covers what a single-location business actually needs to check. <T id="se-ranking" /> earns its place once a business is provably running more than one address: its <Price tool="se-ranking" plan="core" /> plan includes three Local Marketing Plus locations for tracking Google Business Profile rank by address, proof that the reviews coming in are actually moving the needle on each site's own map listing. A narrower alternative is worth knowing about too: <T id="semrush" /> sells a Local Toolkit add-on, priced at $30 a location a month rather than as a full SEO plan, that posts to Google Business Profile and replies to reviews automatically, without the broader keyword-tracking and site-audit tools a local business rarely opens.

## Where most advice gets this wrong

Most round-ups size a local-service stack by headcount, the same way they would size an office team, and miss the number that actually moves this business's bill: how many physical locations it runs. GoHighLevel's Starter plan caps out at exactly three sub-accounts, HighLevel's own term for a separate location workspace, before the plan jumps to <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="unlimited" /> a month, which lifts that cap for good. SE Ranking's trap runs the other way: Core and the <Price tool="se-ranking" plan="growth" />-a-month Growth plan above it both include exactly three Local Marketing Plus locations, so a business that assumes the bigger, pricier plan buys room for a fourth location is wrong; only SE Ranking's own per-location add-on lifts that cap, not a plan upgrade. A business can grow from one person to fifteen at a single address and never cross either line; the same business can cross both the moment it opens a fourth location, whatever its headcount happens to be. Generic advice that sizes this stack off a solo-versus-small-team-versus-growing-team ladder, without asking how many doors the business has, either underbuys a multi-location chain stuck on a Starter plan built for one address or oversells a single busy clinic a rank-tracking plan it would open once a quarter.

## What should a growing, multi-location business budget for next?

A business that has opened a second or third location is the one this stack's growing figure describes: it runs <Stack id="local-service" size="growing" /> a month, adding SE Ranking's Core plan to GoHighLevel and Squarespace's Core plan. Buy in this order. Month one, before there is real booking volume to prove out, GoHighLevel's Starter plan and Squarespace's Basic plan are the whole stack. Add SE Ranking only once a second location opens, since a single address gets nothing from a rank tracker that a free Google Business Profile does not already cover. Watch the fourth-location trigger closely on both tools: past three locations, GoHighLevel needs its Unlimited plan, while SE Ranking's cheaper move is Core's $18.40-a-month extra-location add-on, not a jump to <Price tool="se-ranking" plan="growth" />, which buys project capacity built for agencies running many clients rather than one company's fourth address. Squarespace's own upgrade path is different and simpler: move from Basic to Core the moment a third person, not a third location, needs to edit the site.
