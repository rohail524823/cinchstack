## What does an early-stage SaaS company actually need running?

A SaaS company selling software by subscription runs on five things at once, whether it's a solo founder or a team of fifteen: a marketing site that explains the product and holds the content search traffic depends on, a CRM that tracks trial signups and demo calls without losing them to a spreadsheet, an email tool that nurtures a trial into a paid plan, an SEO tool that finds and tracks the keywords a prospect actually searches, and a shared place for onboarding runbooks, specs and meeting notes once there's more than one person to read them. <T id="framer" /> covers the Site layer at the lowest cost we found for this business; <T id="pipedrive" /> and <T id="activecampaign" /> split the sales-and-nurture job into a pipeline tool and a lifecycle-email tool rather than one bundled platform; <T id="ahrefs" /> handles keyword and backlink research; and <T id="notion" /> holds the docs. None of the five is optional for long: a subscription business without a pipeline loses track of trials, and one without search visibility ends up paying for every visitor through ads instead.

## Is Ahrefs worth it before you have a full-time content writer?

Yes, and earlier than most advice suggests. <T id="ahrefs" />'s Lite plan costs <Price tool="ahrefs" plan="lite" /> a month for one user, 750 tracked keywords updated weekly, and its own crawler-based backlink index, plus API access included from this plan up, useful once an engineer wants SEO data inside an internal dashboard rather than a browser tab. <T id="semrush" /> is the broader alternative: its SEO plan costs <Price tool="semrush" plan="seo" /> a month, and its Content, Social and Local toolkits sit under the same login if the marketing plan grows past search alone, plus a 7-day trial Ahrefs doesn't offer at all. ## Where most advice gets this wrong

Most "software stack for a SaaS startup" lists treat SEO as a problem for later, something to add once there's a full-time marketer or a content budget, and put the CRM and email tools first instead. That ordering is backwards for this business specifically. A CRM only pays back once there are demo calls to track, but search rankings compound for months before they show up as trials, and a B2B buyer's research cycle is long enough that a comparison page published in month one is still bringing in trials in year two. Waiting on SEO until the team "feels ready" just hands that head start to whichever competitor started publishing first, and starting is cheap: <Price tool="ahrefs" plan="lite" /> a month on Ahrefs' Lite plan, or nothing at all on its free tier for a verified domain, is a smaller bill than most founders assume an SEO tool costs.

The second mistake is picking the CRM by brand recognition instead of by what a pre-revenue team actually does all day. HubSpot is the name every SaaS founder already knows, so it becomes the default pick before anyone checks what the free tools actually cover — 2 users, 1,000 contacts, no marketing SMS — against what a real sales process needs once trials start converting. A dedicated pipeline tool billed per seat, not per contact tier, is the more honest starting point, and it's the one this stack picks.
