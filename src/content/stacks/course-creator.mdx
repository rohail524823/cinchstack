## What a course creator's stack actually needs to do

A coach or course creator is really running three jobs at once: delivering the content — a course, a coaching program, a membership — collecting the money, and staying in touch with the people who might buy next. Everything past that is optional until the business is big enough to need it.

The stack below leans on one platform to handle the first two jobs together, a second tool to keep the audience relationship going by email, and a third for the creator's own planning. It skips a separate website builder, a CRM and an SEO tool that most software round-ups add by default, because <T id="systeme-io" /> already includes a basic website, and the other two are not worth paying for until the business hits a specific, real limit (see what to skip, below). For planning course scripts, outlines and standard operating procedures, <T id="notion" /> is free for one person and stays out of the way until there is an actual team to coordinate.

## Which platform should hold your courses, coaching and checkout?

Systeme.io is the strongest fit for most solo and small course businesses. Its plans bundle a funnel and website builder, a course player, communities, a booking calendar and unlimited email sends, and it takes 0% of every sale on every plan, including Free. Startup, at <Price tool="systeme-io" plan="startup" /> a month, covers up to five courses, five websites and 5,000 contacts; Webinar, at <Price tool="systeme-io" plan="webinar" /> a month, adds evergreen webinars, which is how a lot of course launches are actually run.

<T id="kajabi" /> is the clear alternative once the business has steady revenue. Its plans cost far more at every size — <Price tool="kajabi" plan="basic" /> a month for its cheapest tier alone — but they remove Systeme.io's per-funnel and per-automation caps, include unlimited landing pages and marketing emails on every plan, and add a native community plus a dedicated coaching product called Backstage. Kajabi itself calls Backstage a Beta feature with pricing that can still change, so a coach relying on it for client delivery should keep a fallback in mind. Kajabi also takes no cut of sales, charging only its own card-processing rate instead. See [our Systeme.io vs. Kajabi comparison](/compare/systeme-io-vs-kajabi/) for the full breakdown.

<T id="clickfunnels" /> sits between the two on price and fits a business built around paid-traffic funnels and a store more than around course content itself. Its entry-tier Launch plan includes only one workspace sub-account; running more than one offer or brand from a single login needs Scale, at <Price tool="clickfunnels" plan="scale" /> a month, which adds four more. Systeme.io already includes unlimited sub-accounts on every plan, so multi-brand management alone is not a reason to pick ClickFunnels over it — the real case for ClickFunnels is a funnel-and-store business, not a course-first one.

## Does a coach need different tools than a pure course creator?

Not fundamentally, but the priority shifts from a course player to scheduling and live delivery. A missed call or a double-booked slot costs a coaching business a client in a way a slow course-library page does not. Systeme.io's booking calendar, included on every plan, covers scheduling for one-on-one or group calls alongside the same courses, community and email used for async content. Kajabi's Backstage product is built specifically for one-on-one and small-group coaching delivery, with one client seat included on every plan, but again, it is still Beta.

Whichever platform a coach picks, treat scheduling, reminders and a clear cancellation policy as things to test before the first paying client, not features to configure later once something has already gone wrong.

## Do you need a separate website, or does your course platform already have one?

Most course-creator round-ups recommend a website builder and a course platform as if they were two separate purchases. They rarely need to be: Systeme.io's own plans already include a website and a blog, so a course creator does not need a second site-builder subscription on top of it. Paying for both is the most common overspend in this category.

The real reason to add a dedicated site is content marketing. If the plan is to publish a blog on a real schedule and build search traffic over time, <T id="squarespace" /> is worth the extra bill: every current Squarespace plan allows up to 1,000 pages, and Core and above drop the two-contributor cap that limits its cheapest tier. Add it once you are actually publishing on a schedule, not before you have anything to publish.

## Which email tool actually fits a creator's list?

<T id="kit" /> is built specifically for people selling their own content and products, not for agencies managing client lists. Its free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited landing pages, forms and broadcasts, and Creator, at <Price tool="kit" plan="creator" /> a month, adds the automations and email sequences a real course launch runs on.

Kit's real catch: it does not publish a price above 1,000 subscribers on any page you can reach without an existing account, so the entry price is not a reliable guide once your list grows past that point. Budget for an increase, and check the calculator on Kit's own pricing page once you are close.

If a known price at every list size matters more than Kit's creator-specific features, <T id="activecampaign" /> publishes a rate for every contact tier and bundles a light CRM for tracking students or coaching leads, though its own public price is annual-billing only. <T id="mailchimp" /> is the safer, more widely recognized choice if you would rather use software that is not built around a niche.

## Where most advice gets this wrong

Most "start free" advice for course creators treats a platform's free plan as free with no expiration date. Systeme.io's Free plan is genuinely capable — a real course, a real funnel, unlimited email sends, no card required — but its terms cap it at $10,000 in lifetime sales revenue, a limit that does not appear on the pricing page itself. Cross that line and you get a 3-day window to upgrade before your checkout pages stop taking new payments; nobody charges your card automatically, but you also cannot keep selling. A creator running an actual launch to an engaged list can cross $10,000 in total sales faster than most "start free" advice implies, especially once a second product is added to the same account.

The fix is not to avoid the free plan; it is to treat that $10,000 figure as a launch-planning input, not a footnote you discover after a sale bounces. If you can already estimate that your first launch will sell through $10,000 in total, start on Startup, at <Price tool="systeme-io" plan="startup" /> a month, on day one instead. Paying that from the start costs far less, over a real launch, than moving a live, selling course to a paid plan mid-week because the free tier stopped taking payments.
