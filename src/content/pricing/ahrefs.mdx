## Which Ahrefs plan does a freelancer or small team need?

Ahrefs sells six tiers: a free account, then Starter, Lite, Standard, Advanced and Enterprise, and the plan that fits is decided by tracked keywords and user count, not team size. Ahrefs Free never expires and needs no card, but it only covers Site Explorer, Site Audit and Web Analytics for websites you verify, with no Keywords Explorer and no Rank Tracker — fine for auditing your own site, not for research. Starter, at <Price tool="ahrefs" plan="starter" /> a month, looks like the entry point, but it tracks only 50 keywords in one unverified project, caps out at one user with no way to add another, and blocks exports; it suits a personal blog, not a paying SEO engagement. Real solo client work needs Lite at <Price tool="ahrefs" plan="lite" /> a month, covering a freelancer tracking around 500 keywords across a few sites at <Real tool="ahrefs" size="solo" /> for the real bill above. A two-person team tracking closer to 1,500 keywords needs Standard instead, since Lite tops out at 750 tracked keywords; add a second login and the bill lands at <Real tool="ahrefs" size="small" />. A four-person team tracking 5,000 keywords needs Advanced, running <Real tool="ahrefs" size="growing" /> with three extra users added — exactly Advanced's ceiling, with no room to grow before Enterprise.

## Why does an Ahrefs bill grow faster than the plan price suggests?

Every Ahrefs plan below Enterprise includes exactly one user, and a second person is the first thing that moves the number. On Lite, an extra user is metered by credits: free up to 5 credits a month, $20 as a "Casual" user up to 100 credits, or $40 as a "Power" user past that. Standard, Advanced and Enterprise instead charge one flat rate per extra user regardless of usage: $60, $80 and $100 a month. None of it is optional past one seat, and Ahrefs bills it automatically — unprepaid users are charged pay-as-you-go, and the charge applies even if that person is removed before the billing period ends. Beyond seats, Ahrefs sells its other research tools as separate subscriptions: AI Content Helper starts at $99 a month for 50 documents, Report Builder's higher limits cost another $99, and Project Boosts (faster crawling, auto-indexing, one-click title fixes) run $20 or $200 a month per project. Brand Radar, Ahrefs' AI-answer tracking tool, is priced separately again: looking up any brand's AI visibility starts at $199 a month, and tracking your own prompts costs $50 to $250 depending on volume.

## What does Lite's 1,000-credit limit actually mean day to day?

Lite is currently the only Ahrefs plan billed this way. Every user gets 1,000 credits a month, spent by opening reports, applying filters or pulling extra rows in Site Explorer, Keywords Explorer and Ahrefs' other research tools; Site Audit and Rank Tracker don't touch this allowance, so auditing a site or watching rankings never runs a Lite account dry. Standard and every plan above it removes the cap entirely, giving every active user unlimited fair-use credits — the real reason a research-heavy two-person team is often better off on Standard before the keyword count even forces the issue. Running low mid-month on Lite means buying more: Ahrefs prices extra credits at $50 per 500, sold pay-as-you-go (off by default) or prepaid for the year. Nowhere on Ahrefs' pricing or help pages is there a published price for tracking more keywords than a plan's cap allows, only a note that more can be bought in batches of 500 through support — worth asking about before assuming Advanced's 5,000-keyword ceiling is the end of the road.

## Is paying Ahrefs annually worth the switch?

On Lite, Standard and Advanced, usually, if the budget can absorb one large charge instead of smaller monthly ones. Ahrefs bills annual subscribers for 10 months instead of 12, dropping the effective price to <Price tool="ahrefs" plan="lite" field="annualMonthly" /> a month on Lite, <Price tool="ahrefs" plan="standard" field="annualMonthly" /> on Standard and <Price tool="ahrefs" plan="advanced" field="annualMonthly" /> on Advanced. Ahrefs' pricing page calls that a saving of "up to 17%," while its own help articles describe the identical discount as "~20%" — both describe the same 10-months-for-12 arithmetic, so treat either figure as a description, not a number to plan a budget around. Starter never gets this option; it renews monthly only, with no annual price published. Enterprise flips the trade-off around: its plan card reads "Annual commitment required," and Ahrefs' help center confirms a minimum one-year subscription, so the <Price tool="ahrefs" plan="enterprise" /> monthly rate is really a payment schedule inside a year-long contract, not month-to-month pricing. One more catch applies whichever plan a subscriber picks: moving an existing monthly subscription to annual billing is not self-serve — both the pricing FAQ and the help center say to contact Ahrefs support instead.

## What does Ahrefs' pricing page not make obvious?

The biggest one is the trial that doesn't exist: Ahrefs' help center says plainly, "We don't provide any free trials," and its pricing FAQ adds that Ahrefs never runs sales, seasonal or otherwise, or special pricing for non-profits. The only way to try the product without paying is the free account, which excludes Keywords Explorer and Rank Tracker entirely. Refunds are just as narrow: Ahrefs does not issue them as policy, though a monthly subscriber can ask for one with no material account activity, at Ahrefs' discretion. There is no way to pause a subscription — canceling takes effect at the end of the billing period, and the account then drops to the free tier. Rank Tracker applies its own limit regardless of plan: keywords refresh weekly by default everywhere, including Enterprise, so a business that needs daily movement won't get it from Ahrefs at any price. Two costs sit outside every number on the page: sales tax or VAT is added for customers in 38 US states plus Washington, D.C., and in Canada, the EU, the UK and other countries from Switzerland to Japan and Australia. Anyone paying in a local currency is converted at an exchange rate Ahrefs sets and can change, so a renewal can land at a different amount with no list-price change.
