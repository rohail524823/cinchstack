## Which Framer plan fits a freelancer, an agency or a SaaS startup?

Framer names freelancers, agencies and SaaS startups as its best fit, and each tends to land on a different plan. A freelancer publishing one lean site, a portfolio or a single client project with one editor, fits comfortably on Basic at <Price tool="framer" plan="basic" /> a month: two CMS collections and 1,000 items cover a blog or a case-studies section without hitting a wall. An agency running several client sites, or a SaaS startup building a marketing site with a real content plan, tends to outgrow Basic on collections before it outgrows anything else, and moves to Pro at <Price tool="framer" plan="pro" /> a month once a second content type or a second editor is added — the small-team and growing-team bills above show what that looks like in practice. Framer is also explicit about who shouldn't buy it at all: a business that needs a shopping cart has no native way to sell through a Site plan, and a workspace planning to run past 10 total editors has no self-serve plan that allows it, only a custom Enterprise quote.

## Why can Framer's own plan price change without you buying an add-on?

Most software raises your bill by selling something extra on top of the plan. Framer's Basic and Pro cards work differently: <Price tool="framer" plan="basic" /> and <Price tool="framer" plan="pro" /> a month are each plan's price at its lowest AI-credit tier, and the same card carries a dropdown that lets you pick a higher one before you ever subscribe. Pick a higher tier and the plan's own listed price changes immediately — Basic climbs in steps up to $225 a month at its top tier, and Pro climbs up to $1,500 a month at its top tier, both still sold under the same plan name rather than as a separate line item. Credits pay for what Framer calls Agents: AI tools that draft a page, write CMS content or handle localization from a prompt, and a workspace that leans on them heavily can end up paying far more than the advertised entry price for the exact same plan. Anyone quoting Framer's price from a screenshot or an old blog post should check which credit tier it assumes first, since the lowest and top tiers on the same plan can be more than ten times apart.

## What does adding a second, third or tenth editor cost?

Every Framer workspace's first editor, the admin who set the site up, is free on every plan, Free included. Add anyone else who needs full design and publishing access, and Framer charges $30 a month per Editor seat, the same price whether the underlying site is on Basic or Pro. A cheaper option covers people who only touch content: a Content Editor seat, limited to CMS updates, localization and on-page edits, costs $15 a month instead. The three real bills above show what this adds up to: a small team of three editors, the free admin plus two paid seats, runs <Real tool="framer" size="small" /> a month, and a growing team of eight runs <Real tool="framer" size="growing" /> a month, almost all of it seat cost stacked on the Pro plan itself. The one thing seat pricing can't get around is Framer's own ceiling: Basic and Pro both cap a single workspace at 10 total editors, counted across every site inside it, and the only way past that cap is a custom Enterprise quote with no published price.

## Where does Basic hit a wall that Pro doesn't?

Framer's own pricing page labels Basic's limits "Fixed" and Pro's "Flexible, pay what you use," and the difference is real, not just marketing language. Basic is capped at exactly two CMS collections, 1,000 CMS items, 30 site pages and 50 GB of bandwidth a month, with no button anywhere to buy more of any of them — the only way past any of those four ceilings is upgrading the whole site to Pro. Pro starts with more room already, 10 collections, 2,500 items, 150 pages and 100 GB, and then lets you raise each one on its own: an extra 100 pages costs $30 a month up to 700 total, an extra 10 collections costs $50 a month up to 40 total, an extra 10,000 items costs $30 a month up to 40,000 total, and an extra 100 GB of bandwidth costs $50 a month up to 2 TB total. A site that expects real growth, more content types, more pages, more traffic, avoids a forced mid-project upgrade by starting on Pro even when Basic's price looks tempting at first.

## Is switching to annual billing worth it, and can you switch back?

Billed annually, Basic drops to <Price tool="framer" plan="basic" field="annualMonthly" /> a month and Pro drops to <Price tool="framer" plan="pro" field="annualMonthly" /> a month, and making that switch is easy: Framer's help center describes moving a project from monthly to annual billing as instant and self-serve from the Plans tab, with no waiting period involved. Reversing course is not symmetrical. Framer's own documentation says switching an annual site back to monthly billing isn't self-serve at all; it requires contacting support, and whatever value is left on the annual term converts to account credit rather than a refund. Refunds run on a separate clock entirely: Framer refunds a new purchase within 7 days worldwide, or within 14 days for customers in the EU and Turkey, but a usage-based add-on, extra bandwidth or a higher AI-credit tier, stops being refundable the moment any part of it has actually been used. Lock in annual billing once a site's plan and editor count feel settled — switching to it costs nothing up front, but undoing it later costs a support ticket, not a click.
