## Which Squarespace plan fits a freelancer, local business or course creator?

Start with contributors, not features. Every current plan, Basic through Advanced, allows the same 1,000 pages and the same unlimited bandwidth, so page count and traffic never decide which plan to buy. Headcount does. Basic, <Price tool="squarespace" plan="basic" /> a month, caps a site at two contributors including the owner, which fits a solo freelancer or a one-person local-service business with no help yet. The moment a third person needs to log in — an assistant, a co-founder, a hired designer — Basic stops working, and the cheapest plan that fits is Core at <Price tool="squarespace" plan="core" /> a month, which removes the contributor cap entirely and adds custom code plus a first-year Google Workspace inbox. A course creator selling digital downloads should skip Basic regardless of headcount: Basic charges the highest cut of any plan on digital-product sales, and Core's fee on that same category is lower — see the fee ladder below. A freelancer, a local-service business and a course creator get the most out of this lineup; an agency running several client sites does not, for a reason covered further down.

## What actually pushes a Squarespace bill above the plan price?

Two things, mainly: what you sell, and who else needs an inbox. Sell a physical product or a service on Basic, and Squarespace keeps 2% of every sale on top of its own card-processing rate of 2.9% plus 30 cents a transaction; that store fee drops to 0% from Core up, though the card rate only improves on Plus (2.7%) and Advanced (2.5%) — Core pays the same 2.9% as Basic. Sell a digital product, course or membership instead, and the cut is steeper on the cheap plans: 7% on Basic, falling to 5% on Core, 1% on Plus and 0% on Advanced. AI tools are a smaller but real add: Basic gets 10 one-time credits and never more, while Core, Plus and Advanced add 20, 40 or 120 renewing credits a month on top of that same 10 — and once those run out, Squarespace sells extra credit packs from inside the account dashboard at a price it does not publish on any public page. A Google Workspace inbox is free for the first year on Core and up, then renews at $8.40 a month per user; Basic never gets that free year at all.

## Does Squarespace's annual billing really save up to 36%?

Not quite, and the gap is worth knowing before committing a year of payments upfront. Squarespace's pricing page banner advertises "Pay annually to save up to 36%," but its own checkout system reports each current plan's real annual discount as 24% on Basic, 25% on Core, 24% on Plus and 28% on Advanced — meaningful savings, just not the headline number on any of the four plans. Annual billing also throws in a free custom domain for the first year and, on Core and up, that free Google Workspace inbox — both renew at their normal price in year two. The other reason to think before switching is refunds: a new annual plan is refundable in full within 14 days of the first payment, while a monthly plan, and any renewal charge on either billing cycle, is not refundable at all.

## What happens if you run more than one site, or need a third editor?

Squarespace bills per site, not per business. A local-service owner with two locations, or a freelancer maintaining a personal site alongside a client's, pays for two entirely separate subscriptions with two separate logins; there is no bundled multi-site plan or shared team seat pool anywhere on the pricing page. That is also why Squarespace fits an agency managing several client sites poorly: ten client sites means ten full subscriptions, each priced and billed on its own, with no volume discount published for owning more than one. The contributor cap compounds this on Basic specifically: two people, including the owner, is the limit, and each needs their own Squarespace account to log in. A wedding photographer bringing on a part-time assistant, or a consultant who wants a bookkeeper to log in and see the invoicing tools, hits that cap immediately and has to move to Core, whether or not Core's other features matter to them.
