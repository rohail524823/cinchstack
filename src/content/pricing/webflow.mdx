## Is Webflow's cheapest plan really $24 a month?

Technically, yes: the cheapest paid item on Webflow's whole price list is a $24/mo Freelancer Workspace, which is what the box above reports as the "cheapest paid plan." But a Workspace is not a website. It pays for staging sites, AI credits and a place to hold seats; nobody outside your account ever sees it. To publish a real site on a custom domain you also need a Site plan, starting at $25/mo for Basic (no CMS) or $39/mo for Premium, the plan most blogs and marketing sites need because it includes the CMS. Treat the $24 Workspace as an upgrade for people who build sites for clients, not as the entry price for having a website at all.

## Which Webflow plan does an agency, freelancer or SaaS startup need?

Webflow names agencies, freelancers and SaaS startups as its best fit, and each needs a different combination. A freelancer with one client site and a blog needs Premium for that site (<Price tool="webflow" plan="premium" />/mo, or <Price tool="webflow" plan="premium" field="annualMonthly" />/mo billed yearly) and can stay on the free Starter Workspace until several client sites and a free client seat per site make Freelancer Workspace worth its $24. An agency running more sites should move to Agency Workspace (<Price tool="webflow" plan="agency-workspace" />/mo, <Price tool="webflow" plan="agency-workspace" field="annualMonthly" />/mo billed yearly), which adds unlimited staging sites and three free client seats per site instead of one. A SaaS startup's marketing site usually needs Premium too, mainly for its Webflow Cloud allowance: 2 million requests and 30 CPU minutes a month covers a marketing site with a small app bolted on, and usage past that is billed at $2 per extra 1 million requests or $2 per 5 hours of CPU time.

## What actually pushes a Webflow bill up as a team and a site grow?

Two things: editors and traffic. Every Workspace already includes one Full seat for its owner, often the only seat a solo builder ever pays for. Add anyone else who needs to write or design, and Webflow charges $45/mo for a Full seat (Admin, Site admin or Designer) or $19/mo for a Limited seat (Marketer or Content editor) — and a Limited seat can be bought even on the free Starter Workspace, so a small team does not need to upgrade its Workspace plan just to add a content editor. The scenarios above show this directly: the small-team bill's seat cost comes entirely from two Limited seats, and the growing-team bill's from seven. Bandwidth works the other way. Premium includes 50 GB a month, and going past it means buying a tier, from $30/mo for the next 50 GB up to $560/mo for 1 TB. We estimate bandwidth from Webflow's own page-view guidance, treating a visit as roughly one page view at 2-10 MB; the growing-team bill's 1 TB tier is our estimate for about 250,000 monthly visits, not a figure Webflow states directly, so a heavier page could need more and a lighter one less.

## Is paying Webflow yearly worth it?

Usually. Webflow advertises "Save up to 40%" for yearly billing, and the plan prices back that up: Premium drops from $39/mo to $25/mo, Basic from $25/mo to $15/mo, a Full seat from $45/mo to $39/mo, and a Limited seat from $19/mo to $15/mo. Bandwidth add-ons cost less yearly too, by about a third at every tier. The trade-off is commitment. Switching a Workspace between monthly and yearly billing also switches every seat on it to that same frequency, and any upgrade or downgrade is prorated with the credit held as Workspace credit rather than paid back to you. Skip yearly billing only if the site or the team is likely to shrink or change plans soon; lock it in once the setup feels stable.

## What the pricing page does not spell out

A few things only show up in Webflow's help articles, not the pricing page itself. Going over your bandwidth limit is not charged the first month — Webflow calls this surge protection — but going over again the next month triggers an automatic, non-refundable upgrade to a higher tier, and bot traffic counts toward the total right alongside real visitors. Cancelling does not refund the rest of the current term: only a brand-new Site or Workspace plan purchase is refundable, within 14 days, and renewals, seats, add-ons and bandwidth upgrades never are. Ecommerce Standard adds a 2% fee on every sale on top of card processing, a fee Plus and Advanced do not charge. And leaving takes real work: code export needs a paid Workspace plan, the CMS exports one Collection at a time rather than the whole site, and forms stop accepting submissions once you point the domain elsewhere.
