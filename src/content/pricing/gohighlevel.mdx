## Which GoHighLevel plan does an agency actually need?

An agency with three or fewer client accounts can run on GoHighLevel's Starter plan at <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" /> a month; one with a fourth client, or one that wants clients to pay for their own texts, calls and email, needs Unlimited at <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="unlimited" /> a month.

Starter includes unlimited users and contacts and up to three sub-accounts, HighLevel's term for a separate client or location workspace, so a single business running one brand needs nothing more, and a couple of side brands still fit. HighLevel's own FAQ calls this plan "Agency Starter" and pitches it at "a single business owner or a new agency," but Starter cannot rebill any of its usage charges to clients — that only turns on at Unlimited. GoHighLevel, often shortened to GHL, is sold by HighLevel LLC, which calls the product HighLevel in its help center.

Unlimited adds unlimited sub-accounts and lets you rebill usage to clients at cost, with HighLevel fixing that markup at 1.05x to cover the card fees on the agency's own Stripe account. Only <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="agency-pro" /> a month, the top published plan, lets you set your own markup on rebilled usage or turn on SaaS mode to resell GoHighLevel under your own brand and price list. Enterprise pricing exists but only through a sales quote, so it is not something a small team can compare on sticker price alone. ## What actually pushes a GoHighLevel bill above the plan price?

Usage pushes it up: as of <Checked tool="gohighlevel" />, GoHighLevel bills texting, calling, email and most AI from a prepaid wallet on top of the plan, which lifts a 3-user team's Starter bill from <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" /> to <Real tool="gohighlevel" size="small" /> a month.

Every plan's price stops at the software, and the wallet is where the real bill grows. Texts cost $0.00747 per segment, sent or received, plus a carrier fee that runs about $0.0045 for T-Mobile or Verizon and differs for other networks; texting any U.S. number also needs A2P 10DLC registration, a one-time $22.50 fee plus a running monthly campaign fee as low as $1.50 for light senders. Calls add up faster: $0.0166 a minute outbound and about $0.0117 a minute inbound, plus a $0.0018-per-minute conference charge that HighLevel applies to every outgoing call, not only transfers, because it routes every call as a conference. Email through HighLevel's own sending system costs $0.675 per 1,000 messages, and that counts every message you receive as well as send. AI is the widest range: an AI Employee costs a flat $50 or $97 per sub-account a month with fair-use limits, or, without that add-on, HighLevel charges by the token and the minute — Conversation AI on GPT-5 runs $1.25 per million input tokens, for instance. None of this is optional once a location is texting or calling, and all of it draws from the same wallet. ## Is GoHighLevel's annual billing worth it?

Yes, once you are sure of the plan: GoHighLevel's annual billing charges ten months' price for twelve, so Starter works out to <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" field="annualMonthly" /> a month, billed as $970 for the year.

Unlimited drops to <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="unlimited" field="annualMonthly" /> a month ($2,970 for the year); Agency Pro drops to <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="agency-pro" field="annualMonthly" /> a month ($4,970 for the year). The discount applies only to the subscription — texts, calls, email and AI still draw from the wallet at the same per-unit rates either way, so annual billing shrinks the fixed part of the bill, not the usage part that grows with the team. The catch: a month-to-month plan can be canceled from the dashboard at any time, but moving an existing annual plan back to monthly means contacting HighLevel directly rather than clicking a button. If you are still testing which plan and which sub-account setup fits, start monthly and switch once you are sure.

## What does GoHighLevel's pricing page leave out?

GoHighLevel's pricing page leaves out how its wallet recharges, its no-refund rule and two add-on commitments, and it is vague about cards on the trial.

Whether the 14-day trial on the pricing page asks for a card up front is not stated plainly; that page only says billing starts automatically when the trial ends unless you cancel first. HighLevel's Bootcamp trial page, the 30-day route behind the trial button at the end of this page, does list a payment step in its signup form. Every fee is non-refundable under HighLevel's Terms of Service, including the subscription itself and any resold usage. The wallet recharges itself automatically whenever the balance runs low, and HighLevel's "Smart Adjustment" feature can raise that recharge amount on its own after it triggers a few times in a week, unless you turn the setting off; money left in the wallet more than 30 days after you cancel becomes HighLevel's, not yours. HighLevel's own help articles even disagree with each other on when a 5% markup applies to pass-through carrier and registration fees — one article says it always applies, another says only when client rebilling is switched on. Two add-ons also carry commitments the plan price does not hint at: HIPAA compliance, once turned on, cannot be turned back off, and online listings management carries a 3-month minimum term. ## Who should not buy GoHighLevel?

Three kinds of buyer should skip GoHighLevel: one who wants a simple CRM, one who needs a fixed monthly bill, and one who only needs email marketing.

GoHighLevel is built for agencies and local-service businesses that want a CRM, funnels, calendars and messaging under one login, not for buyers chasing the lowest bill for a single feature. Skip it if you want a simple CRM and have no plans to build your own funnels or automations: the $97 minimum plan pays for tools you will not touch. Skip it if you need a fixed, predictable monthly bill, since heavy texting, calling or AI use turns the wallet into the largest line on the invoice and there is no cap to opt into instead. And skip it if all a business actually needs is email marketing; that business would be paying the $97 floor for a CRM, funnel builder and phone system that sit unused.
