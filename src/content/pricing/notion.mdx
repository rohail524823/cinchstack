## Does a one-person Notion workspace ever need to pay?

Not on price alone. Notion's Free plan gives a single-member workspace unlimited pages and blocks, and the <Real tool="notion" size="solo" /> scenario above reflects exactly that: one seat, no charge, no billing cycle to manage. Notion pitches Free as being for "individuals to organize personal projects and life," and at that size its real limits sit elsewhere: 5 MB per file upload, 7-day page history, up to 10 external guests, and only a "Limited Trial" of Notion AI.

The moment a workspace adds a second member, pages and blocks become "limited," and Notion never publishes the number. Inviting a guest to comment on a document doesn't trigger this, since guests aren't counted as members; adding a co-founder, a virtual assistant or a second freelancer does. A solo operator about to bring someone else into the workspace should plan on paying <Price tool="notion" plan="plus" /> a month per member from that point, rather than waiting to hit an unpublished wall.

## Which plan does a 5-person or 15-person team actually need?

Both the small-team and growing-team bills above land on the same plan: <Real tool="notion" size="small" /> for 5 seats and <Real tool="notion" size="growing" /> for 15 seats are each priced on Plus, at <Price tool="notion" plan="plus" /> a month per member. Plus carries no published seat ceiling, and because pages, blocks, file uploads and guest invitations are all unlimited there, nothing about growing from 5 people to 15 forces a move to Business by itself.

Business, at <Price tool="notion" plan="business" /> a member, earns its price only when the team needs something Plus flatly doesn't offer: Notion Agent, AI Meeting Notes, Enterprise Search, SAML SSO, private teamspaces, or 90 days of page history instead of Plus's 30. That's a real fit for an agency running client teamspaces that must stay invisible to each other's staff, a SaaS startup that wants AI search across its own documentation, or any workspace ready to rely on Notion AI rather than trial it. Everyone else is paying for a badge: Notion marks Business "Recommended" on its own pricing page, which is a marketing default, not a sizing verdict for any one team.

## What actually pushes a Notion bill above the seat price?

Two things, and both sit above Plus. Custom Agents and Workers, available only on Business and Enterprise, run on Notion credits billed at $10 per 1,000 credits a month, and the pricing page never says how many credits the free trial includes or how many a typical agent run burns through. A team planning to lean on Custom Agents should treat that line as an open question, not a rounding error, until it has run one inside its own workspace.

Publishing a Notion site on a custom domain also costs extra, on Plus and up, billed "per domain" with no amount shown anywhere on the pricing page. Neither charge touches a workspace that sticks to docs, databases, forms and internal collaboration, which covers most of what the plans above are priced for.

## Is Notion's yearly discount as good as it advertises?

Notion's pricing page opens on the yearly view and says "Save up to 20% with yearly." The seat rates it actually publishes work out to about 16.7% off: Plus runs <Price tool="notion" plan="plus" field="annualMonthly" /> a member billed yearly against <Price tool="notion" plan="plus" /> month-to-month, and Business runs <Price tool="notion" plan="business" field="annualMonthly" /> against <Price tool="notion" plan="business" />. Twenty percent is the ceiling Notion advertises, not the rate most teams will actually get.

The bigger catch is what yearly billing does to a shrinking team. Once a paid seat is added, Notion keeps billing it for the rest of the current billing period even if that person leaves the next day, and on an annual plan the "rest of the period" can be most of a year. A workspace expecting to add and drop contractors, or unsure of its headcount in six months, is safer paying monthly until it settles, then locking in the annual rate once the seat count is stable.

## What does Notion's pricing page leave you to find out the hard way?

A handful of numbers that matter never make it onto the page. The exact page-and-block cap that kicks in once a workspace passes one member isn't published, only described as "limited." The free trial of Notion AI on Free and Plus has no stated length or usage cap. Custom Agents get a free trial of unstated size before the credit meter starts. And the currency selector on the pricing page suggests Notion prices by region, but only the US-locale USD prices have been checked as of <Checked tool="notion" />, so whether a team billing from another country pays the same rate is unconfirmed.

None of that changes what a US-billed team pays today. It does mean a workspace planning to push hard on Notion AI or Custom Agents before upgrading, or one billing from outside the US, is working with real gaps in what Notion discloses up front.
