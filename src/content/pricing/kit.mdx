Kit's pricing page states one clear number per plan, then stops: past 1,000 subscribers, the number lives in a calculator built for a human visitor, not for us. What we can verify, what we cannot, and how to get your own figure make up the rest of this page.

## Which Kit plan fits a solo creator, and when do you need Pro?

Kit's three tiers turn on two questions: do you need automations, and how many people touch the account. The free Newsletter plan, <Price tool="kit" plan="newsletter" /> a month for as long as you stay under 10,000 subscribers, covers unlimited broadcasts, forms, landing pages and selling digital products. Kit's Help Center says it excludes Visual Automations, email sequences, Rules/RSS campaigns and AI Connectors at any subscriber count. A creator who only sends manual newsletters, with no automated sequences, can stay on Newsletter past 1,000 subscribers and pay nothing.

The moment you need automations, Creator is the entry point, at <Price tool="kit" plan="creator" /> a month for up to 1,000 subscribers, whether you have ten subscribers or a thousand. Kit positions Creator for one or two people managing the account, which is exactly the solo bill above. Once a third person needs their own login, Kit's own Creator-vs-Pro guidance points to Creator Pro at <Price tool="kit" plan="creator-pro" />, which adds unlimited team members plus advanced deliverability reporting, subscriber engagement scoring, Facebook Custom Audiences sync and a newsletter referral system, most of which a solo creator will never touch.

## Why doesn't Kit publish its price above 1,000 subscribers?

Every figure so far is Kit's starting-tier price, up to 1,000 subscribers. Past that point, Creator and Creator Pro rise through further subscriber tiers, shown only inside the interactive calculator on kit.com/pricing, not in any of the 400-plus Help Center articles we reviewed. When we tried to load that calculator directly, on <Checked tool="kit" />, a Cloudflare bot-check blocked every method we tried, and the pricing table's own client-side code pulls its live numbers from an account page that requires a logged-in session. We did not create a Kit account solely to read a price list.

One breakpoint above the starting tier is confirmed: Kit's Creator-vs-Pro article puts Creator at $59 a month at 3,000 subscribers, up from $39 at 1,000, the only rung Kit states outside the blocked calculator. That shows the price climbs in defined steps; it does not show the price staying near $39 or $79 once your list passes 1,000. We say this plainly: our small-team and growing-team bills above, both pinned to $79, are the least Kit could possibly charge at 10,000 and 50,000 subscribers, not a real quote. Treat any number above 1,000 subscribers, on this page or anywhere else, as a floor, never as what you will actually pay.

## How do you get your real Kit price at 10,000 or 50,000 subscribers?

Kit's calculator blocks scripts, not people. The fastest way to see your real number is to open kit.com/pricing yourself, in an ordinary browser, and enter your subscriber count on the Creator or Creator Pro card; that figure, not $79, is the one to budget against. If you'd rather not commit to anything first, both paid plans carry a 14-day free trial with no card required, so you can add subscribers up to your real count and read the price straight from Account & Billing.

Whichever path you take, know the mechanics first. Kit's account upgrade and downgrade help article explains that crossing your tier's subscriber limit moves you to the next tier automatically: on monthly billing, that means the new tier's price plus a prorated charge on your next bill; on annual billing, you pay the prorated difference right away, then the full new annual price at renewal. Kit does not reverse this on its own if your list shrinks back down; you switch tiers yourself, from Account & Billing, once you qualify, and the lower price starts on your next billing date.

## What actually pushes a Kit bill higher as you grow?

Kit bills by confirmed, active subscribers, not by how large your list has ever been or how many emails you send. Subscriber count is the one dial that moves your bill within a plan.

Two thresholds matter more than the count itself. The first is 10,000 subscribers: cross it and the free Newsletter plan is no longer available, whatever features you use, since Kit's free tier applies only under that number. The second is team size: Creator suits one or two people, and a third person who needs account access calls for Creator Pro, regardless of subscriber count. A five-person team on a 1,000-subscriber list should budget for Creator Pro on team size alone, not subscriber count.

Kit has no published add-ons, usage fees or extra costs beyond the plan price. The only other charge worth knowing about is a small, temporary hold Kit's payment processor places on your card when you add or change one, cleared automatically within about a week.

## Is Kit's annual billing worth it?

Kit's annual billing gives you 12 months of Creator or Creator Pro for the price of 10, but Kit has not published a fixed monthly-equivalent figure for either plan, so compare your own checkout total against the monthly price yourself before committing a year upfront. Because the annual price already carries that discount, Kit's standard 30-day money-back guarantee is the only refund you get; Kit will not refund or pro-rate any payment made more than 30 days before you ask.

Annual billing does not freeze your tier either: Kit's billing help article confirms that growing past your subscriber tier on an annual plan charges the prorated difference immediately, then the full new annual price at renewal, the same upward-only mechanic as monthly billing. Annual pricing rewards a creator confident their list will hold steady; a list you expect to double this year will still trigger a mid-year charge, discount or not.
