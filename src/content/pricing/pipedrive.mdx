## Which Pipedrive plan does a growing sales team need?

Pipedrive sells four plans, and which one a business needs comes down to two things: whether it wants automation and a synced inbox, and whether it wants lead capture, project tracking and e-signatures bundled in rather than bought separately. <Price tool="pipedrive" plan="lite" /> a seat a month buys Lite, the cheapest plan and the one built for a single rep who just wants to log calls and move deals through a pipeline by hand: it has no connected email inbox and no automation of any kind, not even one automated step. The first real step up is <Price tool="pipedrive" plan="growth" /> a seat a month for Growth, which adds a synced mailbox per seat, up to 50 shared automations and 5 Pulse nurture sequences — the minimum plan for a team that wants Pipedrive to follow up on its own. <Price tool="pipedrive" plan="premium" /> a seat a month buys Premium, the first tier where LeadBooster's chat and web-form lead capture, the Projects add-on and Smart Docs e-signatures are all included at no extra cost, along with basic data enrichment for company records. <Price tool="pipedrive" plan="ultimate" /> a seat a month is Ultimate, worth it mainly for the 500 monthly enrichment credits that cover people as well as companies, a sandbox test account and extended phone support hours; a team that doesn't specifically need those three is paying for headroom it won't use. Every plan bills strictly per seat, whether or not the person assigned to that seat is active, so the real lever on cost is headcount, not any usage cap.

## What actually pushes a Pipedrive bill above the seat price?

The seat price never includes email marketing. Campaigns, Pipedrive's own bulk-email tool, is a separate add-on on every plan, Ultimate included, and it's billed by how many marketing subscribers you hold, not how many emails you send: the Standard tier starts at $0.016 per subscriber a month and steps down as the list grows. That single add-on is why the bills above run higher than the seat price alone — a solo rep on Lite pays <Real tool="pipedrive" size="solo" /> a month once Campaigns is added. LeadBooster (chatbot, live chat, web forms and 10 monthly Prospector lead-reveal credits) costs $39 a company a month, the Projects add-on costs $20 a seat a month and Smart Docs starts at $39 a month — but only on Lite and Growth; all three come free with every seat on Premium and Ultimate, which is the real reason those two plans cost more up front but can end up cheaper once a team needs more than one of them. Web Visitors, which identifies the companies browsing your site anonymously, starts at $49 a month for up to 200 identified organizations and steps up to $99 and then $299 a month as traffic grows, and it's sold as a separate add-on on every plan too, including Ultimate. Two smaller usage charges round things out: extra Prospector lead-reveal credits, once you've used the 10 that come with LeadBooster, start at $1 per credit, and extra data-enrichment credits, available only on Premium and Ultimate, start at $0.10 per credit.

## Is paying Pipedrive annually worth the commitment?

Every plan costs less per seat when billed annually, but not by the same amount. Lite drops from <Price tool="pipedrive" plan="lite" /> to <Price tool="pipedrive" plan="lite" field="annualMonthly" /> a month, a cut of about 42%, the steepest of the four. Growth and Ultimate both drop by about 20%, and Premium by about 25%. Pipedrive's own pricing page rounds this up to "discounted up to 42%," a figure that's accurate only for Lite. The catch that headline doesn't mention: annual subscriptions are prepaid for the full year and are not refundable. Cancelling one doesn't return any money already paid; it keeps your access running until the paid term ends, then simply stops renewing. A monthly plan, by contrast, can be cancelled at any time and stops billing at the next date. Annual billing is worth it once you're confident in the plan you've chosen; before that, the flexibility of monthly billing is worth more than the discount you'd be giving up.

## What does Pipedrive's pricing page not spell out?

A few things take digging to find. Smart Docs' price bands are labeled only by an internal "units 1-30" and "units 31-100" range that no page defines; it's most likely a monthly signature-request count, since the add-on's own free trial is metered in 5 signature requests, but Pipedrive never states that plainly. Web Visitors works in reverse: its price is public up to 2,000 identified organizations a month, but above that Pipedrive assigns a custom tier only after running a 7-day trial of your actual site traffic, so you can't know the real monthly cost until after you've already committed to the trial. Seats bill strictly by headcount: adding a new user with no free seat available automatically adds, and bills, another one, so an over-provisioned account can quietly grow its own bill. And Pipedrive's back-end pricing data lists paid Nova AI SKUs priced per seat, even though every public page about Nova says plainly that it's included free on every plan; Pipedrive doesn't explain the discrepancy anywhere, so there's no current reason to expect a Nova charge, but it isn't a fully settled question either. Accounts still on Pipedrive's pre-2025 plan names — Essential, Advanced, Professional or Enterprise — may also be paying different prices than the Lite, Growth, Premium and Ultimate rates quoted on the current page.

## How does Pipedrive's real cost compare with Zoho CRM, HubSpot and GoHighLevel?

<T id="zoho-crm" /> is the closest comparison: it's also a per-seat sales CRM, and its Standard plan costs <Price tool="zoho-crm" plan="standard" /> a month per user, undercutting even Pipedrive's cheapest plan, and it folds a limited amount of bulk email into that price instead of selling it as a separate add-on. <T id="hubspot" /> takes a different approach: its Marketing Hub is free for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts, a real option Pipedrive doesn't offer at any size, since Pipedrive has no free tier at all, only a 14-day trial. HubSpot's paid Starter plan, at <Price tool="hubspot" plan="starter" /> a seat a month with no long-term commitment, would otherwise suit a team the size of Pipedrive's small-team scenario — but HubSpot sells marketing SMS only as an add-on to Professional, which requires a 12-month commitment and a one-time $3,000 onboarding fee. A small team that also wants that SMS add-on sees its real HubSpot bill run to <Real tool="hubspot" size="small" /> a month, far above Pipedrive's <Real tool="pipedrive" size="small" /> for the same size team, even though Pipedrive has no SMS product to sell that team at any price. <T id="gohighlevel" /> bundles a CRM, funnels and a phone system into one flat plan starting at <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" /> a month, and it can bill texts and calls by usage on top of that — something Pipedrive simply cannot do at any price, because Pipedrive has no SMS product at all.

## Who should skip Pipedrive, and what should they use instead?

Skip Pipedrive if bulk email or SMS needs to be included in one subscription: Campaigns is always a separate bill here, and there's no SMS product to buy at any price, so a business built around texting leads should look at <T id="gohighlevel" /> instead, where messaging is at least metered rather than absent. Skip Lite specifically, even though it's the cheapest plan, if the team wants Pipedrive to send follow-up emails or run automations on its own; Lite has neither, and Growth is the real floor for that. Hold off on buying Web Visitors until you've run its 7-day traffic trial, since committing based on the published entry price alone means finding out your real tier and bill only after the fact. And for a business that mainly wants the plainest possible per-seat CRM price, with a little bulk email folded into the base plan instead of billed separately, <T id="zoho-crm" /> is worth checking before committing to Pipedrive.
