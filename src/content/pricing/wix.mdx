## Which Wix plan does a freelancer, local business or course creator actually need?

Start with what the site needs to do, not how big the business is. Wix's free plan builds and hosts a full site with no credit card and no time limit, but it publishes only to a Wix subdomain, keeps Wix's own branding on every page, and sits outside Wix's detailed plan-comparison table entirely. The moment a real business wants its own domain, the free plan stops being an option, and the cheapest paid tier, Light, becomes the entry point at <Price tool="wix" plan="light" field="annualMonthly" /> a month billed annually. Light removes Wix branding, adds a custom domain with a free domain for the first year, and allows 2 site collaborators including the owner — enough for a freelancer or a local-service owner working alone. Light's real limit isn't storage or collaborators, though: it excludes online payments, customer accounts and every ecommerce tool outright. A course creator or store owner needs Core at <Price tool="wix" plan="core" field="annualMonthly" /> a month instead, which is also where the collaborator cap rises to 5. An 8-person team, or any business that wants automated sales tax and loyalty tiers with point expiration, needs Business at <Price tool="wix" plan="business" field="annualMonthly" /> a month — the plan Wix marks "Recommended" on its own pricing page. Business Elite, at <Price tool="wix" plan="business-elite" field="annualMonthly" /> a month, only earns its price once a site genuinely needs 100 collaborators or unlimited storage; little else on that tier justifies the jump by itself.

## Why won't Wix show you a real monthly price?

This is the catch that matters most on this page. Every price on wix.com/plans is the annual, paid-in-full rate shown as a monthly-equivalent figure. Wix's Help Center confirms the company also sells true month-to-month and multi-year Premium plans, but says plainly that "the final price can be seen on the purchase page, before payment is completed" — meaning nowhere public. You only see the monthly rate after logging in, choosing a plan, and selecting Monthly at checkout instead of Yearly. Some independent pricing guides publish an estimated month-to-month number anyway; we won't repeat any of those figures here, because they disagree with each other, none of them links to a Wix-owned page, and Wix could change the real number at any time without telling anyone who copied down someone else's guess. What we can say for certain: paying annually means one upfront charge rather than twelve smaller ones — <Price tool="wix" plan="light" field="annualMonthly" /> a month on Light works out to $204 charged upfront for the year, and <Price tool="wix" plan="business" field="annualMonthly" /> a month on Business is $468 upfront. Switching to monthly billing, whatever that rate turns out to be once you see it, forfeits the free first-year domain and the bundled premium apps that come with an annual plan. Wix also runs no free trial of any paid plan; its Help Center says directly that new Premium purchases instead carry a 14-day money-back guarantee if the plan turns out to be wrong.

## What pushes a Wix bill above the plan price?

Card processing is the biggest variable for any site that takes payments. Wix Payments charges 2.9% plus 30 cents a transaction on domestic US cards, more for American Express, PayPal, and other alternative methods, plus an extra 1.5% on any cross-border card. Separately, Wix's "Pricing Plans" feature — its name for subscriptions, memberships and other recurring billing — carries its own service fee on top of that: 4% of each charge on Core, 2% on Business, and 0% on Business Elite; Light doesn't offer Pricing Plans at all, so the fee never applies there. That fee is specific to recurring billing: ordinary one-time product and service sales carry no separate Wix commission beyond the card-processing rate above. A chargeback costs $15 outright, and more when the disputed payment ran through PayPal. A domain bought outside the free first year that comes bundled with an annual plan has its own price on Wix's Domains page, and that page shows only first-year rates, not what a renewal costs later. One more variable has nothing to do with features at all: Wix's Help Center says Premium prices vary by country without saying by how much for any of them, so a business outside the US should expect a different number than everything quoted on this page.

## What happens when an agency manages several Wix sites?

Badly, compared to a single-site business. Wix bills every site as its own subscription with its own login, and the collaborator cap attached to each plan — 2 on Light, 5 on Core, 10 on Business, 100 on Business Elite — applies per site, not per account. An agency running ten client sites isn't paying for one plan with ten sites attached to it; it's paying for ten separate subscriptions, each chosen and billed on its own, with no bundled multi-site discount published anywhere on wix.com/plans. That also rules out pooling collaborator seats across sites: a designer working on six client sites needs to be added as a collaborator on each one separately, and every site still counts that designer against its own cap. For a single-site freelancer or local-service business this pricing model causes no trouble; for an agency juggling many client properties, the bill and the login count both grow in lockstep with every new site it takes on.
