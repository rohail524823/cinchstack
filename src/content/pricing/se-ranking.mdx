## Which SE Ranking plan fits a freelancer, agency or local-service business?

SE Ranking sells two self-serve plans, Core and Growth, plus a custom Enterprise tier with no published price. The decision usually comes down to keyword volume and project count, not team size. An agency managing several client accounts usually adds a second login almost immediately, which brings the bill to <Real tool="se-ranking" size="small" /> once the extra-seat add-on is included. Once daily keyword tracking needs to pass 2,000 a day, the choice is between Growth and Core's own extra-keywords add-on: Growth costs <Price tool="se-ranking" plan="growth" /> a month for three seats and 5,000 keywords tracked daily, running <Real tool="se-ranking" size="growing" /> for a four-person team that adds one more seat on top of what Growth includes. Enterprise sits above Growth with no self-serve number at all — SE Ranking's pricing page describes it only as "custom limits and pricing," a sales conversation instead of a checkout page.

## Does hiring a second person always mean the next SE Ranking plan up?

Not necessarily, and that's easy to miss on a pricing page built around two tiers. Core's $129 already covers 10 projects and 2,000 keywords a day, well above what a one- or two-person operation typically needs, so a second or third teammate can often be added to Core itself for $20 a month each rather than forcing an upgrade. Two extra seats on Core, at $20 a month apiece, still add up to less than Growth's $279 a month, even though Growth's higher price buys three seats outright plus 5,000 keywords a day and 30 projects. What actually forces a move to Growth is the keyword or project ceiling, not the head count: past Core's 2,000-keywords-a-day default, the only ways up are Growth's flat $279 or Core's own extra-keywords add-on, which starts at $22.40 a month for the smallest block and adds capacity in small increments rather than doubling it in one jump. Count keywords and projects before counting people; a bigger team doesn't automatically mean the next plan.

## What billing rules make SE Ranking's annual discount less flexible than it looks?

Two things make the headline 20% annual discount less flexible than a simple percentage suggests. First, it's charged as one upfront payment for the year, not smaller discounted monthly charges — SE Ranking's own FAQ says the only way around that lump sum is a quarterly-payment schedule arranged directly with sales. Second, the discount doesn't reach everything on the bill: the Agency Pack and the API add-on are sold on annual billing only, even though Core and Growth themselves still offer month-to-month billing. Free assisted migration from another SEO platform is another annual-only perk; a monthly subscriber gets self-serve import tools instead. Whether an extra $20-a-month seat gets the same 20% cut when the main plan is billed annually is not stated anywhere SE Ranking publishes. A separate catch sits outside the discount question: SE Ranking's own "Compare plans" table lists a lower starting price for extra manager seats than its Help Center FAQ states outright, and this page uses the FAQ's explicit $20 figure because it says plainly what a seat costs — what the table's lower number actually buys is never explained. Purchased extra keywords run on their own separate billing cycle from the main subscription, and a subscription left unrenewed for more than 60 days gets its account data deleted rather than just paused. Payment runs through Stripe only; PayPal isn't accepted, and accounts still billed through the older 2Checkout system are being migrated during 2026. SE Ranking also runs a one-time "Solo Plan" outside Core, Growth and Enterprise: a single payment for 3 months of reduced-limit access (150 keywords a day, one user, no AI or agency add-ons).

## What does the 14-day trial actually let you test?

SE Ranking's 14-day trial needs no credit card, but what it hands you isn't identical to what Core provides afterward. The trial runs at higher limits than Core alone: 10 projects, 3 manager seats, 750 keywords tracked daily, 20 AI prompts a day, AI competitive research across 3 domains, 50,000 export rows, plus access to the Agency Pack and the AI Search add-on for the full 14 days. Two things stay switched off regardless: buying extra seats, keywords or locations, and MCP access. The catch worth knowing before the trial ends: those 3 trial seats don't carry over. Core includes just one manager seat, so a team that used all 3 seats during the trial either drops to a single login on Core or pays $20 a month for every seat it wants to keep past the first. Subscribing to Growth avoids that step down, since its three included seats match the trial — but $279 a month is a bigger jump than most solo trial users need.

## How does SE Ranking's price compare with Ahrefs and Semrush?

SE Ranking's Core plan, at <Price tool="se-ranking" plan="core" /> a month, tracks 2,000 keywords a day for one seat — more keywords than Ahrefs' Lite plan at the same <Price tool="ahrefs" plan="lite" /> price (750 a week, not a day) and more than Semrush's entry SEO plan at <Price tool="semrush" plan="seo" /> (500 a day). A second user costs $20 a month more on Core, $45 a month more on Semrush's SEO plan, and either $20 or $40 a month on Ahrefs' Lite plan depending on how many research credits that person burns through — Ahrefs' cheaper Starter plan doesn't allow a second user at any price. AI-search visibility tracking is bundled generously into both SE Ranking plans by default, 100 or 250 tracked prompts a day; Semrush's plain SEO plan leaves it out entirely, and adding Semrush's AI Visibility Toolkit afterward costs another $99 a month. Ahrefs' Lite plan includes only 5 tracked AI prompts a day in Brand Radar, and its fuller AI Visibility Index is sold as its own separate subscription. Choose SE Ranking when a growing team's seat count is the real cost driver and AI-search tracking should come bundled in from the start; choose Ahrefs for weekly-updated research priced by user credits instead of team size, or Semrush for daily tracking across a larger site worth paying per seat for.
