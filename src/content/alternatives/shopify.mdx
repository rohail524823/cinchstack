## Who should stay on Shopify instead of switching?

<T id="shopify" /> earns its price in one specific setup: a brand that designs, sources or manufactures a physical product and sells it mainly through its own storefront, not through a booking calendar or a drip-fed course. Every paid plan bundles hosting, checkout, abandoned-cart recovery and automatic sales tax into one bill, so a seller can launch without hiring anyone to run a server. Route payments through Shopify Payments, Shop Pay or PayPal Express Checkout and Shopify adds no transaction fee on top of card processing, which alone offsets a lot of what the picks below solve for. A team that fits inside Grow's staff cap of 5 logins, or a bigger one inside Advanced's cap of 15, has no reason to switch over headcount alone either. If your catalog is mostly physical goods, your team fits those caps and you already use Shopify Payments, what follows probably costs more than it saves. Everyone else, keep reading.

## What actually pushes sellers to look for a Shopify alternative?

The reasons above repeat for a handful of concrete reasons, not vague dissatisfaction. Basic, the plan behind the <Price tool="shopify" plan="basic" /> sticker price in Shopify's own ads, ships with zero staff accounts, so hiring one part-time helper already means paying for Grow before you've sold a single extra order. Route a payment through anything other than Shopify Payments and Shopify layers its own cut on top of the processor's fee: 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow, 0.6% on Advanced, for as long as that gateway stays connected. Sellers whose real product is a course, a coaching program or a membership run into a platform built around items to browse, not a curriculum to release. The same mismatch hits a business that's mostly billing for appointments and services: a catalog with a checkout doesn't replace an invoice or a booking calendar. Some sellers just don't want a platform holding the code, the host or a cut of a sale, on principle rather than over one bill. And the one plan Shopify prices at <Price tool="shopify" plan="agentic" />, Agentic, can't publish a storefront or take an order through it at all, which surprises anyone who assumed a free tier meant a free store.

## What should you check before you cancel Shopify?

Two mechanics matter more than whichever plan you switch to. First, check whether the login you actually need is a full staff account or a collaborator account: Shopify's collaborator accounts, the kind agencies and freelancers use, don't count toward Basic's, Grow's or Advanced's staff-account limits, so a bookkeeper or a contract developer may not force you onto Grow at all. Second, Shopify's plan charges are generally non-refundable, so canceling partway through a billing cycle doesn't buy back the days you stop using it. If your store is less than six months old, confirm whether it's still earning Shopify Rewards credits first: new merchants earn 1% back on sales through Shopify Payments (0.5% through other providers) for their first six months, up to a cap that rises with the plan, and canceling forfeits whatever you've built up so far.

## How do you move a store off Shopify?

Treat the move as a project with an overlap period, not a single cutover day. Start with the product catalog: it's the part almost any platform, this one included, can actually export and re-import, so pull that data out first rather than last. Customer accounts and order history carry over less cleanly, and how much of either a new platform can import is worth confirming before you commit to one. A paid Shopify theme is built for Shopify's own templating system, so plan on rebuilding the storefront itself on the new platform rather than assuming the theme travels with you. Build and test the new store's checkout, product pages and any embedded forms before the old one goes dark, then run both side by side for at least one full billing cycle so a customer who lands on the old checkout on launch day still finds one that works. Because the plan itself is generally non-refundable, time the actual cancellation for the end of that cycle rather than partway through it.
