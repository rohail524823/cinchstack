## Who gets more from staying on Notion than from switching?

Notion earns its place for one kind of business more than any other: a team whose real work is writing and organizing, not tracking dozens of dependent tasks. An agency documenting client processes, a freelancer keeping proposals and notes in one place, or a SaaS startup writing specs and onboarding docs gets a flexible page-and-database system for as little as <Price tool="notion" plan="free" /> a month for one person, or <Price tool="notion" plan="plus" /> a seat a month once a second person joins. Guests are also free and unlimited on every paid plan, limited only to the pages someone actually shares with them. None of the alternatives below match that combination of a flexible wiki, a lightweight database and free guest access, because none of them are built to be a wiki first. Switching away from a genuine fit just rebuilds the same page structure somewhere else for no real gain. The rest of this page covers the other two cases: a team that has outgrown Notion's databases for project tracking, and a team using Notion as a stand-in for a job — a CRM, a website — a dedicated tool does better.

## What should you check before you cancel a paid Notion workspace?

Canceling or downgrading a Notion workspace does not cut you off immediately: Notion's own billing rules keep paid features active until the end of the current billing cycle, and a subscription otherwise renews automatically until you act. What takes more care is the money and the content around it. Because a paid seat is billed for the rest of the period it was added in even after a member leaves, time a cancellation for the end of a billing cycle rather than the middle of one, or you pay for seats you have already stopped using. Check your page-history window before you commit to anything, too: Free keeps 7 days, Plus 30, and Business 90, so a page edited incorrectly weeks ago may already be unrecoverable by the time you get around to exporting it. If your workspace has external guests, remember they can only open the individual pages someone specifically shared with them, so a guest losing access when you cancel is not something a dashboard warns you about in advance; tell anyone who depends on a shared page before the workspace actually closes.

## How do you actually get your pages and databases out of Notion?

Start the export while your workspace is still active, not after you cancel. Notion's help center describes exporting an entire workspace as PDF, HTML, or Markdown for pages with CSV for databases, generated from the workspace settings; a full workspace export can take up to 30 hours to process, and the download link it emails you expires after 7 days, so request it with time to spare and check it before your subscription lapses. Two limits catch people off guard: exporting a page bundled together with all of its subpages as a single zip file is available only on Business and Enterprise, so a Free or Plus workspace exports the same content page by page instead of in ready-made bundles, and any database still set to a form view has to be switched to a table view first, since form views do not export at all. Open the exported files before treating them as a backup: confirm the properties, tags and linked databases your business relies on came through, then rebuild the structure in whichever alternative you picked, running it alongside Notion before you cancel either one.

Match the swap to the specific reason, not to whichever name is loudest in a search result. If the real reason is that a Notion database was standing in for a sales pipeline, <T id="pipedrive" /> or <T id="zoho-crm" /> replaces that job properly instead of adding more custom properties to patch it.
