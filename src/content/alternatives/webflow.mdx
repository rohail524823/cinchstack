## Who should stay on Webflow rather than start shopping?

Webflow earns its price for a specific kind of buyer: someone who wants a designer's control over a site's markup, not just its words and images, and who accepts a metered bill in exchange for that control. That description matches <T id="webflow" />’s own strongest customers: an agency or freelancer building sites for paying clients, and a SaaS startup whose marketing site needs custom interaction a template builder can't produce. The Agency and Freelancer Workspaces, at <Price tool="webflow" plan="agency-workspace" /> and <Price tool="webflow" plan="freelancer-workspace" /> a month, are built around exactly that job, bundling free client seats onto each site instead of charging every collaborator out of one shared pool. A content-heavy business already running Premium's 20,000-item CMS, custom code and a Webflow Cloud app is sitting on real, working infrastructure, and rebuilding all of it elsewhere to dodge a seat fee or a bandwidth tier rarely pays for itself. If that sounds like your account, the more useful read is the bandwidth and seat mechanics below, not the rest of this list.

## What is actually driving most searches for a Webflow alternative?

Two billing choices explain most of the searches that land on this page, and neither is a missing feature. Webflow prices a site and its editors as two separate subscriptions: the Site plan covers the pages themselves, at <Price tool="webflow" plan="premium" /> a month for the cheapest plan with a CMS, while the Workspace and every collaborator's seat, $45 a month for full access or $19 for content-only, bill on their own schedule. A three-person team on Premium is already paying at least three separate line items before a single bandwidth add-on gets involved. Bandwidth itself is the other half of the problem: Webflow publishes only a page-view range for each tier, not a fixed per-visit number, so a growing site moves into an add-on tier that is an estimate, not a guarantee, and two consecutive months over the limit trigger a non-refundable upgrade automatically, with bot traffic counted the same as a real visitor. By the growing-team size in the bills above, that combination reaches <Real tool="webflow" size="growing" /> a month on a plan whose own sticker price is a fraction of that.

## What should you check before you cancel Webflow?

Read the refund and renewal terms before doing anything else. Only a new Site or Workspace purchase is refundable, and only within 14 days; a renewal, a seat, an add-on or an automatic bandwidth upgrade is not, so canceling partway through a term does not return the unused part of the month. If the Workspace is billed yearly, switching its billing frequency switches every seat attached to it at the same time, which is easy to miss when the actual goal is downgrading only one thing. Check which subscription is actually holding the site, too: the Site plan and its Workspace are billed and managed separately, and downgrading the Workspace does not touch the Site plan sitting underneath it, so confirm both before assuming a cancellation is complete. If the store runs on Ecommerce Standard, its 2% transaction fee keeps applying to every sale until the plan actually lapses, on top of whatever Stripe or PayPal already charges.

## How do you actually move a site off Webflow?

Start with the CMS, since it is the slowest part to move. Webflow's export tool pulls content out one Collection at a time, so a blog with more than one content type means several separate exports, not one file. Design and markup travel more easily: any paid Workspace plan, not the free Starter Workspace, can export a site's HTML and CSS, giving the new host real material to build from instead of a screenshot. Forms are the part people forget: they stop collecting submissions the moment the site leaves Webflow's hosting, so rebuild and test every form and newsletter signup on the new platform before the old one goes dark, not after. Keep the replacement running alongside Webflow for a full billing cycle, then cancel once the new site is collecting submissions and, if it sells anything, taking orders.
