## Who should stay on Klaviyo instead of switching?

Switching costs the most for exactly the business Klaviyo is built for: a store on <T id="shopify" /> or <T id="woocommerce" /> whose welcome, abandoned-cart and post-purchase flows already read live order and browsing data. Rebuilding that logic on a new platform, with a new integration, is real work, and Klaviyo's free plan means that work never has to happen on a deadline: it runs indefinitely for up to 250 active profiles and 500 sends a month, with a small monthly allowance of SMS and Composer usage included. An agency managing several such stores, or a freelancer running flows for one, sits in the same position: the deeper the store integration goes, the less a generic alternative actually saves once you count the rebuild. If your list isn't doing that job, the case to stay is much weaker.

## What actually pushes people to look for a Klaviyo alternative?

Six frictions come up most, and two of them get a fuller explanation on our <T id="klaviyo" to="hub" /> review, so here's the version specific to comparing what comes next. The price stays the same whether or not your list is actually tied to a store: flows are built to read Shopify and WooCommerce order and browsing history, and the Reviews add-on is priced by order volume, so a newsletter or service list without a product catalog behind it gets little of what that price buys. Composer, Customer Hub, Social Marketing and Reviews are each billed on top of whichever Email tier you're on, so a business that wants all four ends up managing five separate line items, not one. The self-serve calculator shows no usable rate for MMS, WhatsApp or RCS at any list size, so a business that specifically needs one of those channels can't price it in before signing up. And there's no way to try the paid Email plan, or any of its four add-ons, at full strength before paying; the free plan is generous but capped, not a preview of what the paid tiers actually do. The other two frictions, no annual-prepay discount and an automatic move to a pricier tier once you cross your profile limit, are just as real; they're just already covered in depth elsewhere on the site.

## What should you check before you commit to an alternative?

Tally what you're actually replacing before assuming one tool covers it all. An account running Email alongside Composer, Customer Hub, Social Marketing and Reviews is really managing five subscriptions, and Kit or Mailchimp alone only replace the Email piece of that; ActiveCampaign adds a CRM on top of it. Texting works differently across all four: Kit has no SMS product at all, Mailchimp and ActiveCampaign both sell it as an add-on with no flat published rate, and GoHighLevel is the only one that prices texting as ordinary, published usage the way Klaviyo does. Check list size next: Kit's confirmed pricing stops at 1,000 subscribers, with its rate at 10,000 or 50,000 subscribers published only inside its own account-side calculator, so get a real quote before assuming its bill stays low once your list is actually that big. And if your combined Klaviyo spend moved you onto manual, invoiced billing rather than self-serve checkout, confirm that contract's own notice period before you plan a switch date around anything Klaviyo publishes for self-serve accounts.

## Will your flows and history actually move to a new platform?

Export more than a flat contact list if you can: a segment's logic, which events and properties define who belongs in it, is worth documenting separately, since it's unlikely to survive a plain CSV of names and emails. What genuinely will not travel to a new platform is the flow logic itself: welcome, abandoned-cart and post-purchase flows are built on Klaviyo's own read of your store's order and browsing history, so each one needs rebuilding inside whichever tool replaces it, using that tool's own integration with your store rather than an imported file. If Klaviyo's signup forms or pop-ups are embedded on your site, point those at the new platform before the old account closes, so a subscriber on switch day still lands somewhere you're still paying for. Budget time for sending reputation, too: a domain's deliverability history builds up on whichever platform has actually been sending from it, so expect a stretch of lower open rates while the new platform warms up, and plan to run both tools side by side for at least one full send cycle rather than cutting over in a single day.

## Which alternative actually fits the reason you're leaving?

Match the fix to the friction that's actually yours. Sending a newsletter or service list with no store behind it? <T id="mailchimp" /> already bundles purchase-behavior tracking and product recommendations into every plan, including Free, with no ecommerce-specific add-on to buy or skip, and <T id="kit" />'s free plan alone covers up to 10,000 subscribers with no product catalog assumed anywhere in the product. Tired of an annual bill that never earns a prepay discount? Kit gives two free months on annual billing from its very first paid tier, starting at <Price tool="kit" plan="creator" /> a month, and Mailchimp knocks about 15% off its <Price tool="mailchimp" plan="premium" /> Premium plan on annual pre-pay from the start, or off Essentials and Standard once your list passes 10,000 contacts. Want automation and a CRM in one price instead of stacking four separate add-ons? <T id="activecampaign" /> folds marketing automation and a CRM into every paid tier, Starter included, and <T id="gohighlevel" />'s <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" /> Starter plan bundles a CRM, funnels, a site builder and usage-priced email and SMS into one subscription, with only its AI agent billed separately. Need MMS or WhatsApp rates you can check before you sign up? GoHighLevel publishes exact per-message rates for both; Klaviyo's own calculator doesn't. Want to use the real paid product, not a capped free tier, before you commit? ActiveCampaign's 14-day trial needs no card and runs on its Professional-plan features. Worried about losing a month's payment if plans change mid-cycle? Kit backs every paid plan, including annual ones, with a 30-day money-back guarantee. Whichever friction is yours, weigh it against Klaviyo's own numbers on [Klaviyo vs. Mailchimp](/compare/klaviyo-vs-mailchimp/) before you commit to a new platform's minimum term.
