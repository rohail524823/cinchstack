## Who should stick with HubSpot instead of switching?

HubSpot earns its price in one specific setup: a team that wants marketing, sales, service and content tools all writing to the same contact record instead of exporting lists between disconnected systems. That fits <T id="hubspot" />'s own best-fit businesses, agencies running several clients and SaaS startups building a sales motion from scratch, both of which need more than one team looking at the same lead at once. A solo operator with a short list can also stay for free: the free tools cover 2 users and 1,000 contacts with a working CRM, forms and live chat, and Starter removes the branding for <Price tool="hubspot" plan="starter" /> a seat a month once that cap starts to pinch. If your team already runs Professional across several hubs on one shared record, rebuilding that structure elsewhere to save money on a single hub rarely pencils out. Everyone else, keep reading.

## What actually pushes people to look for a HubSpot alternative?

Most people land here for one of six reasons, and each one is specific enough to name. Buying Marketing Hub Professional means paying a required setup fee before a single campaign goes out, $3,000 for Professional or $7,000 for Enterprise, charged automatically, not something you can decline. Sign up for Professional and you're committed for 12 months regardless of billing cadence; Enterprise skips the monthly option entirely and bills annually only. Contacts work the same way in reverse: cross a tier and the higher price applies instantly, and dropping back down by excluding contacts from marketing status waits until your next renewal. Native texting is the feature HubSpot won't sell at all outside the U.S.: Professional and Enterprise carry it, Starter and the free tools don't, and even on Professional it only reaches U.S. and Canadian phones. Starter itself draws a hard line at 10 automated actions for its email automation, thin for anything past a simple welcome sequence, and the only way past it is Professional's much larger bill. Least visible of all, HubSpot prices every Core Seat at whichever hub edition you own that costs the most, so one higher tier of, say, Sales Hub quietly raises what the other marketing seats cost too.

## What should you check before you cancel HubSpot?

Two mechanics matter more here than the sticker price. HubSpot's subscriptions don't just expire on their own: unless you switch off auto-renewal in the Account & Billing section of your account before your current term ends, the agreement rolls you into another term of the same length, up to a year, right back on the terms you were trying to leave. Second, letting a paid subscription lapse doesn't wipe the account the way some vendors' terms do: outside of a for-cause termination, HubSpot's agreement says it will keep the Free Services running once a paid subscription ends, rather than shutting the account off outright. What that means for the seats, workflows and contacts you had above the free tier's caps of 2 users and 1,000 contacts isn't spelled out in the public terms, so don't assume anything above those numbers survives the switch untouched. Ask HubSpot directly, and treat anything past those caps as something to record or export while your term is still active, not after it lapses.

## How do you move your CRM data off HubSpot?

Start the move before your term actually ends, not after. HubSpot's customer agreement lists a "Retrieval of Customer Data" provision that specifically survives termination, so your right to get your own records back doesn't disappear the moment a subscription lapses, though the mechanics aren't published on HubSpot's public marketing pages; ask your account contact how it works and how much time you'll get. HubSpot's Product & Services Catalog confirms CRM data itself can be exported, up to 300 exports a day and 30 a day through the API, so contacts and deals travel with you in some form. Workflows, custom reports and automation rules are account configuration rather than CRM data, and the catalog doesn't list them among what exports, so budget time to rebuild whichever sequences matter most rather than expecting a file to bring them along. If your site runs HubSpot's forms, live chat or a booking widget, point those embeds at the new tool before you cancel, so a lead filling out a form on launch day doesn't land in an account you're about to lose. Run the replacement alongside HubSpot for one full billing cycle before you let the term lapse.
