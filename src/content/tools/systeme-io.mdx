Systeme.io's plans, add-ons and billing rules are laid out in full on its own pricing page. This page covers what the platform actually is, who it fits well, where it comes up short as a product, and which kind of business should buy it, skip it, or build around it.

## What kind of business does systeme.io actually fit?

Systeme.io, built by ITACWT Limited and launched in 2018 out of Dublin, Ireland, puts a funnel builder, an email sender, a course host, a community, a booking calendar and a lightweight CRM behind one login. That is the whole pitch: instead of paying separate bills for a page builder, an email platform and a course host, a solo seller runs all of it from one dashboard and one contact record. It fits a course creator or a freelancer selling a handful of offers particularly well, since both can launch on the Free plan, which needs no card, covers 2,000 contacts, and includes one course for up to 500 students; the [Systeme.io free plan limits](/tools/systeme-io/free-plan/) decide how long that free start lasts. It fits far less well once a business is built around a physical-product catalog rather than a funnel or a course, which gets its own section below. Because so much lives in one account, walking away later means rebuilding funnels, sequences and course content elsewhere, not just canceling a subscription.

## Where does systeme.io genuinely earn its price?

Two things matter more than any single feature. First, systeme.io takes 0% of every sale on every tier, including Free, so the platform's own cut does not grow as revenue does; the only per-sale cost is whatever Stripe or PayPal charges for card processing, and that applies no matter which platform a business uses. Second, every plan, Free included, allows unlimited staff (assistant) accounts and unlimited sub-accounts, with no per-seat add-on to buy. Staff accounts are logins for people who help run the same funnels and courses. Sub-accounts are separate workspaces, useful for a second brand or a client, and each one inherits the exact limits of whichever plan the parent account is on. That distinction matters: a business on Startup can open as many sub-accounts as it wants, but every one of them is stuck at Startup's 10 funnels and 10 automation rules, so the unlimited count is real while the unlimited usefulness is not, unless the parent account is on Unlimited.

## What are systeme.io's sharpest limits?

Three specific caps show up before the plan price does. The evergreen webinar, a funnel type built around a pre-recorded webinar playing on a set schedule, is not available at all on Free or Startup; it only unlocks starting on Webinar, at <Price tool="systeme-io" plan="webinar" /> a month, so a business whose entire offer runs on that one funnel shape has to buy past the entry tier just to build it. Checkout is the second point to check: besides Stripe and PayPal, systeme.io's pricing page lists Apple Pay, Razorpay, Flutterwave, Mercado Pago, Xendit and cash on delivery, but which of them a buyer sees depends on the processor the seller connects and the countries it serves, so confirm your own country before building around one. Third, because subscriptions carry no refund window on any paid plan, the safer order of operations is to build a real funnel on Free, confirm it does what the business needs, and only then pay for Startup, Webinar or Unlimited.

## Who should skip systeme.io?

Three kinds of buyer are better served elsewhere. A seller with a genuine physical-product catalog should look past systeme.io entirely: product variants are capped at just 50 on the Free plan, rising to 100 and 250 through Startup and Webinar, and topping out at 5,000 even on Unlimited, whose own marketing calls contacts and variants unlimited. A catalog-first platform such as <T id="shopify" /> or <T id="woocommerce" />, whose free core plugin carries no such ceiling, fits that business better than a funnel tool with a store bolted on. A business that wants SMS included in its subscription, rather than working only once you open and fund a separate Twilio account, should also look elsewhere; <T id="klaviyo" />, for comparison, meters and bills SMS itself rather than routing it through a connected third-party account. And anyone who might want their money back after paying should know that before subscribing, not after: no systeme.io plan carries a refund window. The [alternatives page](/alternatives/systeme-io/) works through more specific swaps for each case.

## How does systeme.io fit into a course creator's or freelancer's stack?

CinchStack files systeme.io under the sell layer, but its feature list reaches into three others at once: email sending covers the grow layer, its websites and blogs cover part of the site layer, and its CRM pipelines cover a slice of the run layer. In practice, a solo course creator or freelancer can start a business without buying anything else at all, provided contacts stay under the active plan's cap and the automation, tag and pipeline limits above are enough for how the business actually runs. Growth is what forces a decision: once a list outgrows a tier's contact cap, or a business needs more automation rules and CRM pipelines than a tier allows, the next step is either the next systeme.io plan or a dedicated tool bolted on for just that one layer. The [course-creator stack](/stacks/course-creator/) and [freelancer stack](/stacks/freelancer/) both work through that decision in detail; a business built around a physical-product catalog instead should start from the [Shopify-store stack](/stacks/shopify-store/) rather than stretch systeme.io's product-variant caps.

## Which businesses should actually buy systeme.io?

A course creator selling one flagship course or a tight suite of offers is the clearest yes, especially while under the Free plan's 500-student, 2,000-contact ceiling; the same goes for a freelancer running a handful of funnels who wants email, checkout and booking in one place rather than three separate logins. An agency managing several clients can use systeme.io's sub-accounts to do it, but that really only works at the Unlimited tier, since a Startup- or Webinar-level parent plan hands every client sub-account those same tight caps. A local-service business built around bookings, reviews and follow-up calls, or an agency that leans on texting and phone work, is better matched to <T id="gohighlevel" />, which bills that kind of usage directly rather than through a connected account. A store selling a real physical catalog should start with <T id="shopify" /> instead, and an early-stage SaaS company selling subscription software, not funnels or courses, sits outside what systeme.io is built for altogether.
