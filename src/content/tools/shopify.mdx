This page covers who Shopify actually fits, where its flat plan price stops covering the real bill, and where a different tool serves a business better. Every plan, add-on and billing rule is broken out on Shopify's own pricing page.

## Who is Shopify actually built for?

Shopify is a hosted commerce platform: one admin panel runs a store's checkout, hosting, point-of-sale software and order management, and the same account can sell on social platforms and marketplaces, plus list products in AI shopping chats through Shopify Catalog. That combination fits a business whose core job is selling a catalog of physical or digital products, solo or with a small team, whether the selling happens mostly online, mostly in person, or both.

It is worth one caveat on the free plan shown above. The $0 Agentic plan only lists products in Shopify Catalog for AI chat channels and the Shop channel; it cannot publish a working online store or take an order through Shopify's own checkout. Anyone who wants a public storefront needs a paid plan, starting with Basic.

## What makes Shopify's flat fee worth paying?

Shopify's biggest advantage shows up at checkout, not on the pricing page. Pay through Shopify Payments, Shop Pay or PayPal Express Checkout, and Shopify adds no fee of its own on top of card processing. Every paid plan also includes automated sales tax calculation, abandoned-checkout recovery, discount codes and gift cards, so a new store does not need to buy or configure those separately, and casual in-person selling is included too.

The billing itself is more flexible than it looks. Basic, Grow and Advanced can all be paid month-to-month, so a new store is never locked into a year of fees it has not yet earned back, and switching to yearly billing instead trims roughly a quarter off each of those three plans. That optionality disappears only on Plus, which drops the monthly option entirely and requires a 1- or 3-year term.

## Where the flat fee stops being flat

The plan price is only the floor. Basic includes no staff accounts at all, just the store owner, so the moment a store needs even one employee logged in, the bill moves to Grow at <Price tool="shopify" plan="grow" />/mo; collaborator accounts for freelancers and agencies, and POS-only staff, are the login types that do not count against that limit.

Payment processing hides the next catch. Choosing a checkout provider other than Shopify Payments does not just mean paying that provider's own rate: it adds a second, Shopify-specific charge on top, worth 2% of the order on Basic and dropping to 0.6% on Advanced. Because it is a percentage of every sale, that charge scales with revenue rather than shrinking as a share of it the way the flat plan fee does as a store grows. There is a newer wrinkle too: on any store created since May 12, 2025, that same per-order fee reaches into the gift-card or store-credit portion of a sale, and paying the rest of that order through Shopify Payments does not exempt it.

Two smaller limits are worth knowing too. Live carrier-calculated shipping needs Advanced; Grow only gets it with yearly billing or for an extra fee Shopify does not publish, and Basic cannot turn it on at all. And the plan price never includes paid themes or the apps a store adds for reviews, upselling or accounting, which run on their own billing cycles on top of the plan itself.

## Who should look for something else?

Shopify's plans are built around products, orders and shipping, and that shows in who it fits poorly. A course creator or coach whose product is a cohort, a membership or one-on-one coaching is paying for cart and inventory features aimed at a different business; <T id="kajabi" /> is built around course hosting, coaching and a checkout for that model instead. A service business that lives on booking appointments and sending invoices, rather than shipping a catalog, is in a similar position: Shopify has no built-in scheduling or invoicing, so that business would be buying software for a job it does not have.

A business that is mostly a content or marketing site with only a handful of products may be better served starting with a site-first tool such as <T id="squarespace" /> and adding a small store, rather than starting from Shopify's product-first admin. The [alternatives page](/alternatives/shopify/) covers more of these cases and names the specific tool that fits each one.

## How does Shopify fit into the rest of a store's stack?

Shopify covers the sell layer: taking payment, running checkout and fulfilling orders. It also ships a basic marketing tool, Shopify Messaging, with 10,000 free marketing emails a month on every paid plan, which is enough for a genuinely small store's first campaigns.

Once a store outgrows that free allowance, or wants deeper segmentation, automated flows triggered by purchase behavior, or SMS alongside email, the next layer to add is a dedicated marketing platform such as <T id="klaviyo" />, which many Shopify stores add alongside it. A full breakdown of a shopify-store stack, including where to add that layer and what to leave out early on, is at [the Shopify store stack](/stacks/shopify-store/).

## Is Shopify worth it for your store?

For a solo seller with a real product catalog who plans to accept card payments through Shopify Payments, Basic is enough on day one; there is no reason to pay for Grow's staff accounts before there is staff to add. The moment a second person needs a login of their own, move to Grow rather than sharing one login, which blurs who touched which order. A team that outgrows Grow's 5 staff logins, or needs live carrier-calculated shipping without paying extra or committing to yearly billing, belongs on Advanced; POS Pro is an $89-per-location add-on on Basic, Grow or Advanced alike, so running several store locations does not by itself force an upgrade.

Outside that shape, Shopify is the wrong starting point. A course, coaching or membership business fits a platform built around that model instead, and a booking-and-invoice service business fits neither Shopify nor an ecommerce platform generally. For every business actually selling a catalog, Shopify's combination of no added fee under Shopify Payments and one bundled admin for checkout, hosting and point-of-sale is difficult to beat.
