## Who is Klaviyo's active-profile pricing actually built for?

Klaviyo bills its Email plan by active profiles rather than by seats, and that one choice decides who it fits. Klaviyo's own about page describes it as a "B2C CRM" built on unified customer profiles and integrations with commerce platforms including <T id="shopify" /> and <T id="woocommerce" />, and says it has more than 205,000 paying customers in over 100 countries. That framing matters more than any single feature: Klaviyo earns its price by turning purchase and behavior data into segments and automated flows, so it fits a business that already has that data flowing in, and it strains one that doesn't.

The entry paid tier is <Price tool="klaviyo" plan="email-500" /> a month for 500 active profiles. A freelancer or solo creator with roughly 1,000 subscribers pays close to <Real tool="klaviyo" size="solo" /> a month, and a small store or a single-client agency list, usually around 10,000 active profiles, runs close to <Real tool="klaviyo" size="small" /> a month. Watch profile count, not subscriber count: Klaviyo counts anyone its system can reach, including a shopper who abandoned checkout without ever opting in, so a connected store can rack up billable profiles before a single person actually subscribes to anything.

## Where does Klaviyo earn its keep, and where does it strain a small budget?

Two things make the pricing model easier to live with than it first looks. Every paid Email tier includes monthly sends equal to ten times its profile count, so a list that grows in step with how often you email it rarely forces an early upgrade on volume alone. And Klaviyo's free plan has no time limit: it stays free for as long as the account holds at or under 250 active profiles and 500 email sends a month, so a genuinely small list can run on Klaviyo indefinitely at no cost.

The strain shows up on the billing side, not the feature side. There is no annual-prepay discount anywhere in self-serve pricing, a real gap if you're used to a discount for paying yearly elsewhere. Klaviyo also enforces what it calls profile compliance: cross your tier's profile limit and you're moved to a pricier tier at the next billing cycle automatically, whether or not you'd opted into any upgrade preference. The only lever you have is suppressing or deleting profiles before the cycle renews; once you're over the line, the upgrade itself isn't optional. Want faster support or a dedicated strategist instead of what comes standard with any paid plan? That's billed separately too: priority support starts at $500 a month, and an enterprise support tier starts at $1,800 a month, both quoted through Klaviyo's sales team.

## Which buyers should pick something other than Klaviyo?

Skip Klaviyo if you don't have much purchase or engagement data to segment on. Its pricing and its pitch both assume a connected store or a genuinely engaged subscriber list; a local service business with a short client roster, or an early-stage SaaS team billing by seat, ends up paying for a behavioral-data engine it doesn't have enough data to use well. Skip it, too, if you want one flat platform fee: Composer, Customer Hub, Social Marketing, Reviews and SMS are all separate charges on top of Email, each scaled on its own metric, so the Email price alone is never the whole story.

## How painful is it to leave once you're in?

Klaviyo's help center splits "leaving" into three different actions, and they are not interchangeable. Downgrading to free keeps your data and most features, just capped at free-plan limits. Canceling a single paid plan, SMS, for example, turns off that plan's features (any flow with an SMS step stops sending) but leaves the rest of your data alone. Closing your account entirely is the only option that deletes everything, and Klaviyo's own instructions say to export campaign analytics, flow analytics, profiles and templates before you confirm it, since none of it can be recovered afterward.

Timing matters too: canceling immediately rather than at the end of your billing cycle forfeits the rest of that paid month with no refund. On the upside, Klaviyo publishes its own guide for migrating to another email or SMS provider, and account permissions limit who can do any of this by accident: owners or admins can cancel a plan or downgrade to free, but closing the account outright takes the account owner specifically.

## Where does Klaviyo sit in a small business's stack?

Klaviyo occupies the "grow" layer: it earns and keeps customers once a "sell"-layer tool, a storefront, is already in place, not before. For an [online store](/stacks/shopify-store/), Klaviyo usually sits directly behind Shopify or WooCommerce, picking up checkout and purchase events the storefront doesn't act on by itself. For an [agency](/stacks/agency/) running client lists, or a [freelancer](/stacks/freelancer/) building a list of their own, the same active-profile pricing means the bill tracks the size of what you're actually managing rather than a flat per-seat fee that punishes a small operation.

In any of these three, add Composer, Customer Hub or Reviews only once a specific feature is pulling its own weight; none of the three is required to send email or SMS, and starting with Email alone keeps the first bill close to the solo or small-team figures above.
