## Which one actually fits an agency running client accounts?

An agency managing several client accounts is the clearest home for <T id="gohighlevel" />. Starter already includes 3 sub-accounts for <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" /> a month, and moving up to Unlimited at <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="unlimited" /> a month removes that sub-account cap entirely and lets the agency rebill phone and email usage to clients at cost. Agency Pro, at <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="agency-pro" /> a month, adds a marked-up SaaS mode for reselling the whole platform under the agency's own brand; a separate white-label mobile app add-on, also $497 a month, lets an agency put its own name on the mobile app too. <T id="clickfunnels" /> scales the same axis with Workspaces, which rise from 1 on Launch to 20 on Dominate, but nothing on its pricing page or in its terms describes a matching client-rebilling or SaaS-resale feature. That gap matters most for an agency whose business model is charging clients a marked-up monthly fee for access to a branded platform: GoHighLevel is built to support that directly; ClickFunnels is built to run funnels, not to resell itself.

## Which one fits a solo creator selling one offer?

A solo creator selling one course, program or service through a single funnel is the clearest case for <T id="clickfunnels" />. Launch, at <Price tool="clickfunnels" plan="launch" /> a month, already includes a funnel builder, an online store, 3 courses, community and email tools, with no percentage taken from any sale and no usage wallet to top up. <T id="gohighlevel" />'s Starter plan costs the same <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" /> a month, but that price buys a CRM and phone system a solo creator with one offer is unlikely to configure, and texting a customer list at all first requires A2P registration and a running campaign fee before a single message goes out. The two plans cost the same on the label; the difference is what a solo seller has to set up and pay for beyond it. ClickFunnels' 30-day money-back guarantee on payments also gives a new creator more room to change course than GoHighLevel's terms, which call all fees non-refundable.

## How does the bill change as the team or contact list grows?

The two tools grow in different directions. GoHighLevel's plan price is fixed by sub-accounts, not by team size or contact count: Starter's unlimited users and unlimited contacts cover a business of 3 people or 30 without an upgrade, so its bill grows only with texting, calling, email and AI usage. ClickFunnels' plan price is fixed by team-member seats and contacts instead: Launch caps out at 2 seats and 10,000 contacts, Scale at 5 seats and 75,000 contacts, and Optimize at 10 seats and 150,000 contacts, so adding a third staff account or a contact past a plan's cap means upgrading the whole plan, not paying for the increment alone. A team that grows its message volume faster than its headcount will feel that on GoHighLevel's wallet; a team that hires faster than it texts will feel it on ClickFunnels' seat caps.

## Where most advice gets this wrong

The common assumption is that GoHighLevel's metered wallet makes it the pricier, less predictable option next to ClickFunnels' flat tiers. Run each tool's own published cost scenario for a small team and a growing team, and the opposite shows up. GoHighLevel's small-team scenario, 3 users and 5,000 contacts sending a modest amount of email and text, comes to <Real tool="gohighlevel" size="small" /> a month, all on the base Starter plan. ClickFunnels' small-team scenario, sized for 3 staff accounts, needs the Scale plan at <Price tool="clickfunnels" plan="scale" /> a month, because Launch's 2-seat cap is one short of what 3 staff accounts require. The gap holds at the next size up: GoHighLevel's growing-team scenario runs <Real tool="gohighlevel" size="growing" /> a month, while ClickFunnels' equivalent needs the Optimize plan at <Price tool="clickfunnels" plan="optimize" /> a month, because 8 staff accounts exceed Scale's 5-seat cap. The two scenarios are not sized identically: GoHighLevel's assumptions track contacts and texting volume, while ClickFunnels' track staff seats and sales volume. But each is that tool's own standard way of sizing a business, and by that measure GoHighLevel's usage-based wallet adds up more slowly than ClickFunnels' seat-capped tiers once a team passes 2 or 3 people. The metered bill that looks scarier on paper is not automatically the bigger one.

## What does each one leave out?

Each platform has a catch its pricing page does not make obvious. GoHighLevel's prepaid wallet auto-recharges itself whenever the balance runs low, and there is no setting to turn that off; a "Smart Adjustment" feature can even raise the recharge amount on its own if it triggers a few times in a week. HighLevel's terms also call every fee non-refundable, and a canceled account's data is deleted after 90 days, so leaving is not a quick decision. ClickFunnels' catch is less about billing mechanics and more about what is not stated: its current pricing and signup pages do not say how long the free trial runs or whether a card is required to start one, and the pricing page markets "Unlimited Funnels" on every plan while flagging that label "Limited Time," which leaves open whether a funnel cap could return. Neither company's pricing page settles what happens if a ClickFunnels workspace goes over its contact or email-send limit; that is worth confirming directly with ClickFunnels before committing a real contact list to either platform.

## How hard is it to switch later?

Switching between the two is mostly a matter of timing, not a documented export-import path. Neither vendor publishes a tool for migrating directly from the other platform, at least not on the pages checked for this comparison, so moving contacts, funnels or courses across means exporting what each platform allows and rebuilding the rest by hand; confirm the specifics with whichever tool you are leaving before you cancel anything. Leaving GoHighLevel has one concrete cost worth timing around: money left in its prepaid wallet is forfeited 30 days after cancellation unless you request a refund first. Leaving ClickFunnels is more forgiving on paper, with its 30-day money-back guarantee and the ability to change plans at any time, though that guarantee covers payments, not a promise about how easy the data itself is to move. Whichever direction you are switching, cancel the losing tool only once the new setup is live and confirmed working, not before.
