Notion's other advantage is where its AI lives: Notion Agent, AI Meeting Notes and Enterprise Search are bundled into the Business seat price, <Price tool="notion" plan="business" /> a month, with no separate line item. ClickUp's Brain AI and Everything AI are add-ons on top of any paid plan, at $14 or $33 a member a month, charged for every member whether they use AI or not, including a seat a former employee left open. Guests follow the same pattern: Notion's are free and unlimited on every paid plan, limited to the pages they're invited to; ClickUp's permission-controlled guest seats are capped by a formula that scales with paid seats, though view-only guests are unlimited too.

## Where does ClickUp actually win?

ClickUp's advantage is depth of built-in project tracking. Free Forever's own limits confirm Gantt charts, Timeline, Workload views and Dashboards exist from the first dollar, capped at a fixed number of uses; Unlimited removes the file-storage cap and makes Custom Fields, Gantt charts and time tracking unlimited, and Business makes Dashboards, Timeline and Workload unlimited too, plus sprint points and reporting. Notion's plans list none of these as a dedicated view type; its databases add subtasks, dependencies and custom properties, useful for simple project tracking but not a Gantt chart or a workload view across a whole team.

## What does the free plan actually let you skip?

Both free plans are real, not just a trial, and both have a ceiling a growing team hits without warning. Notion Free is unlimited in pages and blocks only while the workspace has one member; once a second person joins, Notion's own comparison table calls pages and blocks "limited" without publishing the number. ClickUp's Free Forever has no membership trigger and welcomes unlimited members and tasks from the start, but it caps total file storage at 60MB for the whole Workspace and gives Custom Fields, Gantt charts, Timeline, Workload and Dashboards a fixed number of one-time uses that never come back once spent. Neither company calls this a "catch" on its pricing page; both frame the free plan as generous, and both are right about the parts they chose to publish.

AI is the other free-plan gap on both sides. Notion's Free and Plus plans carry only a "Limited Trial" of Notion Agent, Meeting Notes and Research mode, with no stated length or usage cap. ClickUp's Free Forever gets a 25-use trial of Brain AI for a Workspace of up to 10 people and can't buy an AI add-on until the Workspace upgrades to a paid plan. A business that wants AI built into daily docs or tasks, not a trial, pays for it on both tools, just on different plans.

## What does it cost to switch later?

Moving into ClickUp from Notion is the easier direction on paper. ClickUp's help center documents a built-in Notion importer, available on every plan: export the Notion workspace as HTML, upload it, and ClickUp turns Notion pages into Lists inside one Folder and Notion docs into a single nested ClickUp Doc. The same article lists what does not survive the trip: comments, attachments and inline database task comments are dropped, and a Notion wiki does not automatically become a ClickUp wiki, though the imported pages can be converted by hand afterward. Moving the other way has no equivalent documented path; Notion's help center does not list a ClickUp importer, so leaving ClickUp for Notion means exporting tasks and docs and rebuilding the structure inside Notion's databases directly.

Commitment is a smaller switching cost than it looks. Every plan on both tools bills month-to-month or yearly by choice, with no forced annual contract. ClickUp's one friction point: a Workspace can't mix monthly and yearly licenses, and switching from monthly to yearly means contacting support, not clicking a toggle; Notion's pricing page states no such restriction. Whichever tool you leave, budget time to move content out before cancelling: neither pricing page describes what happens to your docs or tasks once the subscription ends.

## Where most advice gets this wrong

Most reviews describe ClickUp as the cheaper tool at scale and Notion as the cheaper tool for a solo user, and treat that as a fixed rule. The real bills above show it is not: Notion is the free option for one person, ClickUp is cheaper at five, and Notion is cheaper again at fifteen. The reason is structural, not a pricing gimmick. ClickUp's Dashboards, Timeline and Workload views are capped by uses per Workspace, a shared pool a bigger team burns through faster the more people rely on it, so growth itself forces the upgrade to Business. Notion's plans cap almost nothing by workspace size; its upgrade triggers are features, such as AI, SSO or private teamspaces, not headcount. A comparison that quotes only the sticker prices, <Price tool="notion" plan="plus" /> against <Price tool="clickup" plan="unlimited" />, misses that the tool cheaper per seat on paper is the one more likely to force a plan change as the team grows, while the tool that looks pricier per seat is the one whose plan holds steady. The question worth asking first is whether the team will lean on Dashboards, Timeline and Workload views hard enough to burn through 100 shared uses, not which plan number looks smaller today.

## Who should pick Notion, ClickUp or neither?

The verdicts above cover all six business types; two deserve more than a one-line pick. A course creator gets a wiki or a task board from either tool, not a place to host lessons, sell a cohort or track student progress, so <T id="kajabi" /> or <T id="systeme-io" /> fits that job better than this comparison. A Shopify store mainly needs a lightweight home for SOPs, supplier details and a content calendar, which Notion covers at a lower cost than ClickUp's heavier, task-first design, at least before the store has enough staff to justify Gantt charts or workload views. A local-service business or an agency running several accounts leans the other way: recurring job checklists, crew assignments and multiple client projects are exactly what ClickUp's Spaces, Lists and views hold, at a lower per-seat price at a small team's size too. A SaaS startup usually fits ClickUp as well, mainly for the sprint points and reporting on its Business plan, a detail Notion's plans don't list. A freelancer with a short client list and no team to coordinate fits Notion's free plan first, and pays either company only once a second person, or a real project-tracking need, shows up.
