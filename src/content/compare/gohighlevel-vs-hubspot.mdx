## Where does GoHighLevel actually win?

GoHighLevel wins on two things a small business feels immediately: seats and texting. Every plan, from <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" /> a month on Starter, includes unlimited users and unlimited contacts, so hiring staff or growing a list never forces an upgrade the way per-seat pricing does. HubSpot, by contrast, charges per seat from the first user and bills marketing contacts in tiers that step up as a list grows. Texting shows the gap more clearly. GoHighLevel bills SMS by the segment on every plan, once a one-time $22.50 A2P 10DLC registration is done; HubSpot doesn't sell marketing SMS below Marketing Hub Professional, at <Price tool="hubspot" plan="professional" /> a month, and only to U.S. numbers. For a business built around client accounts, GoHighLevel's sub-accounts are the other clear win: Starter includes three, and <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="unlimited" /> a month removes that cap entirely, while HubSpot has no comparable structure for running several clients' contacts under one login.

## Where does HubSpot actually win?

HubSpot wins where marketing, sales and service need to share one CRM record, and on how much of its pricing it publishes before a sales call.

HubSpot's advantage is structural, not a line item. Marketing, sales, service and content tools all write to the same Smart CRM record, so a contact who fills out a form shows up as a lead without anyone exporting a list between tools; GoHighLevel has a CRM too, but not the separate sales and service hubs that record spans. HubSpot also publishes its whole cost structure in a public Product & Services Catalog, and its price calculator totals a first year's cost, onboarding fee included, before a sales call — a level of published detail GoHighLevel's own help center doesn't match, where GoHighLevel's own articles on its 5% phone pass-through markup actually contradict each other. And HubSpot's free tools genuinely cost <Price tool="hubspot" plan="free" /> for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts, with non-marketing contacts stored free up to 15 million total, so a large historical list doesn't inflate the bill the way active marketing contacts do.

## What does it cost to change your mind later?

On a monthly plan, leaving GoHighLevel costs little: you cancel from the dashboard, fees already paid (including any yearly plan) are not refunded, and a wallet balance left 30 days after canceling is forfeited unless you ask for a refund. HubSpot Professional cannot be canceled or downgraded during its 12-month term, prepaid fees are not refunded, and the $3,000 onboarding fee buys nothing back.

Leaving GoHighLevel is simple to time and cheap to delay: every plan cancels from the dashboard whenever you want, but HighLevel's terms forfeit any unused wallet balance 30 days after cancellation, and a canceled account's data is deleted after 90 days, so timing the last top-up matters more than the cancellation itself. Leaving HubSpot's Professional or Enterprise tier is a bigger decision up front, not after: the 12-month commitment means there's no cancel-anytime option once you sign, prepaid fees aren't refunded, and the $3,000 (or $7,000 on Enterprise) onboarding fee already paid buys nothing back if you leave early. Growing past a plan is its own kind of lock-in on HubSpot's side too: cross a marketing-contact tier and HubSpot bills the higher tier immediately, and you can't drop back down until the contract renews even if your list shrinks again. Neither vendor's pricing pages describe how contacts or content actually move out if you switch, so that homework happens before you cancel either one, not after. ## Where most advice gets this wrong

Most comparisons frame this matchup as $97 versus $20 and call GoHighLevel the pricier option. That framing skips two things. First, HubSpot's real entry price is $0, not $20: the free tools cover 2 users and 1,000 contacts, so the honest floor-to-floor comparison is GoHighLevel's $97 against HubSpot's $0, not against Starter. Second, and more useful, sticker price isn't what actually decides this comparison for most buyers — texting is. A business that never plans to text leads pays less on HubSpot's free tools or Starter than on GoHighLevel's $97 floor, full stop. A business that does plan to text pays a metered, usually small, amount on GoHighLevel from day one, but on HubSpot it pays for an entire Marketing Hub Professional plan, a 12-month commitment and a $3,000 onboarding fee just to unlock a $75-a-month add-on. Comparisons that lead with the base-plan price bury that fork; the one question worth asking first is whether the business will ever send a marketing text, not which plan number looks smaller.

## Who should choose which one?

Choose GoHighLevel for an agency or local-service business that runs client accounts and texts leads; choose HubSpot for a SaaS startup or a team that wants marketing, sales and service on one shared CRM record.

The verdicts above cover six business types, but two are worth explaining rather than just marking "neither." A course creator gets GoHighLevel's courses feature bolted onto a CRM, not a real course platform, and HubSpot has no course feature at all, so <T id="kajabi" /> or <T id="systeme-io" /> fits better than either one. A Shopify store needs a shopping cart and checkout, which neither tool provides at all; that business should start from an ecommerce platform, not this comparison. Among the businesses where one of the two actually fits, the split comes down to structure more than budget: an agency or local-service business running client accounts and wanting native texting fits GoHighLevel's sub-accounts and usage pricing, while a SaaS startup or agency building a single sales-and-marketing motion on one CRM record, and willing to pay per seat and per contact tier for it, fits HubSpot instead. A freelancer with a short list and no texting need can start on HubSpot's free tools at no cost and reconsider only once a real need, texting, client accounts, or a bigger list, shows up.
