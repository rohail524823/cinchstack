## Which one fits a solo creator or coach selling one offer?

A solo creator or coach selling one course or offer through a single funnel is the clearest case for <T id="systeme-io" />. Its Free plan already includes a funnel builder, one course for up to 500 students, unlimited email sends and a 0% transaction fee, all before a card is ever requested; Startup, at <Price tool="systeme-io" plan="startup" /> a month, raises the ceiling to 5,000 contacts and 10 funnels once the free plan's limits get tight. <T id="gohighlevel" />’s Starter plan costs <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" /> a month, the same as Systeme.io's top Unlimited tier, but that price buys a CRM and phone system a solo creator with one offer is unlikely to configure, and texting a customer list at all first requires A2P registration and a running campaign fee before a single message goes out. The limit worth watching on Systeme.io's free plan is not a feature cap: it is a USD 10,000 lifetime sales ceiling, after which a 3-day window opens before its payment pages stop taking new sales. ## Which one fits a marketing agency managing client accounts?

An agency running several client accounts is the clearer home for <T id="gohighlevel" />. Starter already includes 3 sub-accounts for <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="starter" /> a month, and moving to Unlimited at <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="unlimited" /> a month removes that cap and lets the agency rebill phone and email usage to clients at cost; Agency Pro, at <Price tool="gohighlevel" plan="agency-pro" /> a month, adds marked-up rebilling and a SaaS mode for reselling the whole platform under the agency's own brand. <T id="systeme-io" /> also allows unlimited sub-accounts on every plan, including Free, but each one simply inherits the main account's plan limits rather than supporting rebilled usage, and Systeme.io's own pricing FAQ and help center disagree on how its separate white-label pricing actually works. GoHighLevel also bundles the phone and texting system a local-service or appointment-heavy client needs for follow-up, something Systeme.io does not build in at all. An agency whose clients only need funnels, courses and email, with no calling or rebilling, can still run each one from a Systeme.io sub-account instead.

## Where most advice gets this wrong

Most write-ups frame this as "expensive CRM" against "cheap funnel builder" and rank the two by sticker price alone: $97 against $17, $47 or $97. That framing skips the part that actually decides most of these cases: the two tools are not competing to do the same job. GoHighLevel is a CRM and phone system that happens to include funnels; Systeme.io is a funnel, course and email platform that happens to include a lightweight CRM. A business that needs native calling, texting and client sub-accounts will find Systeme.io cannot do that job at any price, since SMS there means opening a separate Twilio account first. A business that only needs funnels, a course and an email list will find GoHighLevel's $97 floor buys a phone system and CRM sub-accounts it will never touch. The second thing sticker-price framing hides is that Systeme.io's Free plan is not free for a business that actually sells: cross USD 10,000 in lifetime revenue and its payment pages stop taking new sales after a 3-day window, so the honest "how much does this cost" answer for a selling business is Startup's <Price tool="systeme-io" plan="startup" /> a month, not $0.

## What's the catch on each pricing page?

Each platform has a catch its plans table does not spell out. GoHighLevel's prepaid wallet recharges itself automatically whenever the balance runs low, with no setting to turn that off, and HighLevel's terms call every fee non-refundable while deleting a canceled account's data after 90 days. Systeme.io's catch sits in its own terms rather than its comparison table: the Free plan's USD 10,000 revenue cap is not mentioned on the pricing page at all, only in the terms and conditions, and a paid account that grows past its contact or funnel limit gets upgraded automatically after a 3-day notice rather than simply blocked. Systeme.io's own pricing FAQ adds a second layer of confusion: it claims Startup has "unlimited funnels" and that downgrading happens from account settings, while the comparison table caps Startup at 10 funnels and the help center says downgrades go through a support ticket instead. Read the terms and the help center on either platform, not just the pricing page, before committing a real contact list or a real product launch to one.

## How hard is it to switch later?

Neither GoHighLevel nor Systeme.io publishes a straightforward way to import the other's contacts, funnels or courses, so the direction you are switching matters more than the switch itself. Moving toward Systeme.io has one advantage built into the price: signing up for its Unlimited plan, or any annual plan, comes with a free done-for-you migration service, something GoHighLevel does not offer as a matching perk on any of its own plans. Moving toward GoHighLevel has no such help, and leaving it later carries its own timing cost: any balance sitting in its prepaid usage wallet is forfeited 30 days after cancellation unless a refund is requested first, on top of terms that call every fee non-refundable regardless. Systeme.io is gentler on the way out in one sense, since cancellation takes effect at the end of the period already paid for rather than immediately, but a downgrade to a lower tier still needs a support ticket and a custom order form, not a settings toggle. Budget the migration work before committing either way, and keep the losing subscription active until the new one is confirmed running.
