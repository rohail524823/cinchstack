Domain name FAQ
What are the different types of domain names?

Domain names are categorized depending on the type, each serves a different purpose online:

Generic Top-Level Domains (gTLD): These are the most common domains you see, like .com, .net, or .org. They’re versatile and open for general use.

Country Code Top-Level Domains (ccTLD): These two-letter domains represent countries or regions, like .us for the United States or .fr for France.

Generic-Restricted Top-Level Domains (grTLD): Similar to gTLDs but with restrictions. For example, .pro is intended for licensed professionals.

Sponsored Top-Level Domains (sTLD): These domains are managed by specific organizations with eligibility rules. For example, .edu is for accredited educational institutions.

Infrastructure Top-Level Domain (ARPA): This is a specialized domain used for internet infrastructure purposes, like technical networking and routing. It’s not something you’d use for a website.

Premium Domains: These are high-value domain names that are short, memorable or keyword-rich. They’re typically already registered but available for resale at a premium price. Investing in a premium domain can give your brand instant credibility, improve memorability and make your site easier to find.

Choosing the right type depends on your goals—whether it’s building a global brand, targeting a specific country or positioning your site for a professional audience.
